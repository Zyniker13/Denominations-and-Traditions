# The Final Destination of the Christian

Part of [Heaven, the New Earth and the Resurrection of the Body](README.md).

Where does the Christian go at death, and where does he live for ever after
the resurrection: in the highest heaven, or on a renewed earth? What did the
Reformed teach, how does it stand against the Greek and Hebrew, and how do
the Lutheran and Jewish answers compare?

The passages are in
[passages.md](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#the-final-destination-of-the-christian).

## Summary

1. **At death the soul of the believer goes at once to Christ, in heaven.**
   This is common to every Reformed confession and writer gathered here,
   against soul-sleep and purgatory: “received into the highest heavens …
   Besides these two places for souls separated from their bodies, the
   Scripture acknowledgeth none” (Westminster Confession 32.1). The Second
   London Confession (1689) says instead “received into paradise, where they
   are with Christ”.
2. **The final state is the whole man, body and soul, “ever with the
   Lord”.** The confessions say “received into heaven” (Larger Catechism
   90) or “inherit that blessed immortality” (Scots Confession 11), and do
   not say where heaven will be after the judgment.
3. **The older Reformed writers place the final home in the highest
   heaven, above and apart from this world.** Wollebius: “the godly
   elevated into the Heaven of the blessed”; Edwards: the church “shall for
   ever leave this accursed world”; Boston: the kingdom “is not in this
   world”. Watson calls it “the third Heaven”.
4. **Others make the renewed world the inheritance of the saints.** Ussher
   has the glorified “possessing the new Heaven and Earth”; Poole says “the
   whole World will then be the Possession, and Kingdom of the Saints”;
   Hodge that “this renovated earth, is to be the final seat of Christ’s
   kingdom”. Boston knew the view (“some learned divines place this city in
   the earth”) and declined it. Goodwin (1655) has the raised saints reign
   on the renewed earth before the judgment, and afterwards “ever be with
   Christ” in “the old heavens”.
5. **The Greek supports both halves of the Reformed answer, and leans to the
   second.** For the intermediate state: σὺν Χριστῷ εἶναι (Phil. 1:23),
   ἐνδημῆσαι πρὸς τὸν κύριον (2 Cor. 5:8), “today … in paradise” (Luke
   23:43). For the final state the New Testament ends with the city coming
   *down* out of heaven and God's tabernacle with men (Rev. 21:2–3), the
   meek inheriting the earth (Matt. 5:5, the Greek of Ps. 37:11), and “the
   creation itself” set free (Rom. 8:21). The rapture of 1 Thessalonians
   4:17 is a meeting (ἀπάντησις), which does not say which way the company
   then goes.
6. **The Lutheran dogmaticians, who expected the world to be annihilated,
   could only place the final home in heaven**: “The place of their
   happiness is in the Scriptures called Heaven” (Schmid §67).
7. **The rabbinic answer is bound to the land.** “All Israel has a share in
   the world to come”, proved from “for ever shall they possess the land”
   (Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:1); “to inherit the land” was the phrase for a share
   in the future life. Maimonides made the world to come bodiless; the
   Reform platforms kept only the immortality of the soul.

## Two questions: the soul at death, and the whole man at the end

The Reformed teaching has two stages, and the question of the final
destination has a different answer at each.

| Stage | Who | Where, in the Reformed writers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| From death to the resurrection | The soul, without the body | The highest heaven, with Christ (all) |
| After the resurrection and judgment | Body and soul together | The highest heaven (Wollebius, Watson, Boston, Edwards; Goodwin after a reign of the saints on the renewed earth); the renewed heaven and earth (Ussher, Poole, Hodge); not specified (the confessions, Calvin) |

## At death: the souls of the righteous with Christ

### The confessions

- **Westminster Confession 32.1** (and Savoy 31.1): “The souls of the
  righteous, being then made perfect in holiness, are received into the
  highest heavens, where they behold the face of God in light and glory,
  waiting for the full redemption of their bodies … Besides these two
  places for souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth
  none.”
- **Second London Confession 31.1**: “received into paradise, where they are
  with Christ, and behold the face of God in light and glory”.
- **Larger Catechism 86**: “received into the highest heavens, where they
  behold the face of God in light and glory, waiting for the full
  redemption of their bodies, which even in death continue united to
  Christ”. **Shorter Catechism 37**: they “do immediately pass into glory”.
- **Heidelberg Catechism 57**: “not only my soul after this life shall be
  immediately taken up to Christ its head; but also, that this my body …
  shall be reunited with my soul”.
- **Scots Confession 17**: the elect departed are at rest, “not that they
  will sleep and come to any certain oblivion (as some fanatics do
  affirm)”, citing “His words to the thief”.

See [confessions.md](confessions.md).

### The theologians

- **Calvin**, *Institutes* III.25.6: “did not the soul, when unclothed from
  the body, retain its essence, and be capable of beatific glory, our
  Saviour would not have said to the thief” that he would be with him that
  day in paradise. But “to pry curiously into their intermediate state is
  neither lawful nor expedient” (p. 617), and “As to the place of abode, the
  question is not less futile and inept” (p. 618). The “bosom of Abraham”
  means that the departed “are received by the common father of the
  faithful”.
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/continental/calvin/institutes-book-3-chapter-25.md))
- **Edwards**, *True Saints* (1747), p. 8: the souls of the saints “are not
  reserved in some Abode distinct from the highest Heaven; a Place of Rest,
  which they are kept in, till the Day of Judgment; such as some imagine,
  which they call the *Hades of the happy*: but they go directly to Heaven
  it self.”
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/american/edwards/true-saints-present-with-the-lord.md))
- **Hodge**, p. 728, after setting out the views of the fathers on
  paradise: “All we need know is that it is where Christ is; that it is a
  place and state in which there is neither sin nor sorrow”.
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/princeton/hodge/systematic-theology-vol-3-last-things.md))
- **Ussher**, Perkins, Dickson and Vincent say the same, and all deny
  purgatory.

## After the resurrection: heaven, or the renewed earth?

### The highest heaven

- **Wollebius** (p. 301): “The end of the World, is the destruction of this
  visible Universe, after the wicked are thrust down to Hell, and the godly
  elevated into the Heaven of the blessed.” He excepts from the
  destruction the two final places (p. 302): “Neither Hel•, nor the Heaven
  of the blessed shall be destroyed.”
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/continental/wollebius/abridgment-of-christian-divinity-last-things.md))
- **Edwards**, *History of the Work of Redemption* (p. 278): after the
  judgment “Christ and all his church of saints … shall leave this lower
  world, and ascend up towards the highest heavens”, and “Christ's church
  shall for ever leave this accursed world” for “the highest heavens, into
  the paradise of God”. The earth is left as the place of punishment: “this
  world shall be set on fire, and be turned into a great furnace, wherein
  all the enemies of Christ and his church shall be tormented for ever and
  ever” (p. 279).
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/american/edwards/history-of-the-work-of-redemption-period-3.md))
- **Boston**, *Fourfold State* (p. 299): the kingdom “is not in this world;
  it lies in a *better country, that is, an heavenly,*” and on Revelation
  21: “(It is true, some learned divines place this city in the earth: but
  the particulars of the description seem to me to favour those most, who
  point us to the other world for it.)”
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/british/boston/fourfold-state-resurrection-and-heaven.md))
- **Watson** (p. 477): “The *Caelum Empyraeum* the Empyraean Heaven, which
  *Paul* calls the third Heaven: *Vbi* Christ is, there is the Kingdom of
  Glory scituated.”
- **Perkins** (p. 68): the third heaven is the place “in which at the end of
  the world shall all the elect both in bodie and soule, haue perfect ioy”.

### The renewed heaven and earth

- **Ussher** (p. 449) describes the glorified as “seated as Princes in
  Thrones of Majesty, crowned with Crownes of Glory, possessing the new
  Heaven and Earth, wherein dwelleth Righteousnesse”.
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/british/ussher/body-of-divinity-heavens-and-last-things.md))
- **Poole's *Annotations***, on 2 Peter 3:13 (image 591): “Not only the new
  Heaven is mentioned, but the new Earth, because the whole World will then
  be the Possession, and Kingdom of the Saints, who follow Christ wherever
  he goes.” On Revelation 21 (image 621) Poole reports that interpreters
  are “divided in their Opinions, whether what is spoken be to be understood
  of the Church Militant upon the Earth; or of the Church Triumphant in
  Heaven.”
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/british/poole/annotations-last-things.md))
- **Goodwin**, *The World to Come* (1655), p. 25: “God doth take the same
  world that was Adams, and makes it new and glorious … so he takes the same
  world, and makes it *a new world, a world to come.*” But Goodwin's “world
  to come” (Heb. 2:5) is a kingdom of Christ between this world and the
  end: the saints of the first resurrection reign in it on earth for the
  thousand years of Revelation 20, as Revelation 5:10 says, “we shall reigne
  on earth”, and 2 Peter 3:13 promises (pp. 32–33). After the judgment the
  saints are in heaven: “If we take heaven properly, there is new heavens to
  be made, but the old heavens shall continue which was made from the
  foundation of the world, and where we shall ever be with Christ after the
  day of judgment” (p. 33). The thousand-year kingdom is the doctrine that
  Calvin rejects as the Chiliasts' (*Institutes* III.25.5).
  ([text](../../theologians/reformed/british/goodwin/the-world-to-come.md))
- **Hodge** (p. 854): “This earth, according to the common opinion, that is,
  this renovated earth, is to be the final seat of Christ’s kingdom. This is
  the new heavens; this is the New Jerusalem”. And (p. 855): “As the
  resurrection bodies of believers are to be human bodies they must have a
  local habitation”. In the consummation (pp. 859–860) the final abode is
  called a house, a city, “a better country”, and “new heavens and a new
  earth”.

### The confessions

The confessions do not choose. Larger Catechism 90: the righteous “shall be
received into heaven, where they shall be fully and forever freed from all
sin and misery … made perfectly holy and happy both in body and soul”.
Westminster Confession 33.2: “the righteous go into everlasting life, and
receive that fulness of joy and refreshing which shall come from the
presence of the Lord”. Scots Confession 11: “the time of refreshing and
restitution of all things will come, in so much that those who from the
beginning have suffered violence … will inherit that blessed immortality
promised from the beginning”. Belgic Confession 37: “the Lord will cause
them to possess such a glory, as never entered into the heart of man to
conceive.” Heidelberg 58 speaks of “perfect salvation” without a place.

**[inferred]** The Belgic Confession's “burning this old world with fire and
flame, to cleanse it” and the Scots Confession's “restitution of all
things” (Acts 3:21) point toward a renewed world; the Westminster Standards
say nothing of the world's end and speak only of heaven.

## The text

**At death.** [Phil. 1:21–24](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#phil-1-21-24):
τὴν ἐπιθυμίαν ἔχων εἰς τὸ ἀναλῦσαι καὶ σὺν Χριστῷ εἶναι. ἀναλύω is to loose
a ship or strike a tent; “to be with Christ” follows at once.
[2 Cor. 5:6–8](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#2cor-5-1-8): ἐκδημῆσαι
ἐκ τοῦ σώματος καὶ ἐνδημῆσαι πρὸς τὸν κύριον: to go abroad out of the body,
and to be at home with the Lord. [Luke 23:43](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#luke-23-43):
σήμερον μετ᾿ ἐμοῦ ἔσῃ ἐν τῷ παραδείσῳ. [Heb. 12:22–23](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#heb-12-22-23):
the readers “have come” (perfect, προσεληλύθατε) to the heavenly Jerusalem
and to πνεύμασι δικαίων τετελειωμένων, the spirits of the righteous already
made perfect. These texts locate the dead believer by a person (“with
Christ”, “with the Lord”, “with me”), and Christ is in heaven, whom “the
heaven must receive” until the restoration (Acts 3:21). The Reformed
“highest heavens” is a fair summary of them. **[inferred]**

**But 2 Corinthians 5 also sets the body's resurrection above that state.**
Paul does not wish to be “unclothed” (ἐκδύσασθαι) but “clothed upon”
(ἐπενδύσασθαι), “that mortality might be swallowed up of life” (v. 4). The
state of the soul with Christ is better than this life (Phil. 1:23) and
less than the resurrection.

**At the end.**

- [Phil. 3:20–21](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#phil-3-20-21):
  ἡμῶν γὰρ τὸ πολίτευμα ἐν οὐρανοῖς ὑπάρχει, ἐξ οὗ καὶ σωτῆρα ἀπεκδεχόμεθα.
  A πολίτευμα is the commonwealth one belongs to, not where one lives:
  Philippi was a Roman colony whose citizens were citizens of Rome. And the
  Saviour is awaited *from* heaven. **[context]**
- [John 14:2–3](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#john-14-2-3): the
  μοναί are in “my Father's house”, and the promise ends in a person: ἵνα
  ὅπου εἰμὶ ἐγώ, καὶ ὑμεῖς ἦτε.
- [1 Thess. 4:16–17](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#1thess-4-16-17):
  ἁρπαγησόμεθα … εἰς ἀπάντησιν τοῦ κυρίου εἰς ἀέρα. ἀπάντησις is used of a
  delegation that goes out of a city to meet an arriving dignitary and
  escorts him in. **[context]** The verse does not say whether the company
  then goes up or comes down; it ends πάντοτε σὺν κυρίῳ ἐσόμεθα.
- [Heb. 11:10, 16](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#heb-11-10-16):
  the patriarchs, promised the land, looked for “a city which hath
  foundations” and “a better country, that is, an heavenly” (ἐπουρανίου),
  heavenly in kind and origin.
- [Rev. 21:2–3](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#rev-21-2-3): the
  city is seen καταβαίνουσαν ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ ἀπὸ τοῦ θεοῦ, and the voice
  says ἰδοὺ ἡ σκηνὴ τοῦ θεοῦ μετὰ τῶν ἀνθρώπων, καὶ σκηνώσει μετ᾿ αὐτῶν,
  the language of the tabernacle (Lev. 26:11–12). The movement at the end
  of the book is from heaven to earth.
- [Matt. 5:5](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#matt-5-5) and
  [Ps. 37:11, 29](../../scripture/last-things/passages.md#ps-37-11-29):
  κληρονομήσουσι τὴν γῆν is the Septuagint of Psalm 36:11 (Swete's text has
  the article as Matthew does). The Hebrew is עֲנָוִים יִירְשׁוּ־אָרֶץ, “the humble
  shall possess the land”, and verse 29 adds “for ever”.

## The Lutheran answer

Schmid §67 ([text](../../theologians/lutheran/heinrich-schmid/doctrinal-theology-last-things.md)):
“The place of their happiness is in the Scriptures called Heaven” (p.
656), and Gerhard: “By the name Heaven, that certain place (ποῦ) is to be
understood, in which the blessed will see God” (p. 662). Among the
“external blessings” of eternal life Quenstedt lists “a most beautiful and
magnificent abode” (p. 662). On the soul at death Schmid §63 agrees with
the Reformed: the soul's lot is happy or unhappy “immediately thereafter”,
and both soul-sleep and the Roman “five different places” are rejected
(pp. 624–625).

**[inferred]** Because the dogmaticians expected the world's substance to be
“reduced to nothing” (§66; see
[new-heavens-and-new-earth.md](new-heavens-and-new-earth.md)), the final home
could not be a renewed earth. The older Reformed writers who also placed
it in the highest heaven agree with them on the destination, and some
(Wollebius, Edwards) on the fate of the earth too.

The Augsburg Confession (XVII) condemns those “who are now spreading
certain Jewish opinions, that before the resurrection of the dead the godly
shall take possession of the kingdom of the world, the ungodly being
everywhere suppressed”: an earthly kingdom of the saints *before* the
resurrection, not a renewed earth after it. Goodwin's reign of the raised
saints on earth falls between the two.

## The Jewish answers

- **The Mishnah** (Sanhedrin 10:1, Rodkinson, p. 265): “All Israel has a
  share in the world to come”, as Isaiah 60:21 reads, “And thy people—they
  will all be righteous, for ever shall they possess the land”. The
  *Jewish Encyclopedia* (1905): “The very term used to express the idea of
  sharing in the future life is ‘to inherit the land’ (Ḳid. i. 10; Matt. v.
  5, after Ps. xxxvii. 11; Sanh. xi. 1, with reference to Isa. lx. 21).”
  ([Mishnah](../../theologians/rabbinic/mishnah-and-talmud-on-the-world-to-come.md);
  [JE](../../theologians/jewish-modern/reform-judaism-on-immortality-and-resurrection.md))
- **Kohler** (1918) adds that the rabbis held the dead would be raised in
  the land of Israel, those buried elsewhere passing “through cavities under
  the earth, until they reach the soil where the miracle of the resurrection
  will be performed” (pp. 394–395).
- **Rav**, Berakhot 17a (Cohen, p. 112): “In the world to come there is
  neither eating nor drinking; no procreation of children or business
  transactions; no envy or hatred or rivalry; but the righteous sit
  enthroned, their crowns on their heads, and enjoy the lustre of the
  *Shᵉkīnāh*”.
- **The Talmud's heavens** (Hagigah 12b): the souls of the righteous are in
  Araboth, the seventh and highest heaven.
- **The Second Temple books**: 1 Enoch 45:4–5, “I will transform the earth
  and make it a blessing: And I will cause Mine elect ones to dwell upon
  it”; 2 Baruch 51:10 (p. 84), “For in the heights of that world shall they
  dwell”; Josephus's speech at Jotapata, that the souls of the righteous
  “obtain a most holy place in heaven, from whence, in the revolutions of
  ages, they are again sent into pure bodies”.
  ([text](../../theologians/jewish-antiquity/second-temple-on-heaven-and-resurrection.md))
- **Maimonides**, *Mishneh Torah*, Repentance VIII: “The World to Come
  harbors neither body nor aught of a concrete form, save only the souls of
  the righteous divested of body as are the ministering angels” (p. 431).
  ([text](../../theologians/jewish-medieval/maimonides/world-to-come-and-resurrection.md))
- **Reform** (Pittsburgh, 1885, art. 7): “We reject, as ideas not rooted in
  Judaism, the beliefs both in bodily resurrection and in Gehenna and Eden
  (Hell and Paradise) as abodes for everlasting punishment and reward.”

Fuller treatment is in [jewish-sources.md](jewish-sources.md).

## Assessment

**The intermediate state.** **[inferred]** The Reformed doctrine that the
believer's soul is at once with Christ is what the Greek says, and the
Lutheran dogmaticians say the same. “The highest heavens” adds a location,
which follows from Christ's being in heaven (Acts 3:21; Heb. 9:24). The
Second London Confession's “paradise, where they are with Christ” keeps
closer to the words of Luke 23:43 and Philippians 1:23.

**The final state.** **[inferred]** Here the Reformed are divided, and the
texts favour the later view. The older writers took 1 Thessalonians 4:17
(“caught up … in the air”) as the beginning of an ascent to the highest
heaven, and Revelation 21 as a figure for it, as Boston says. But
ἀπάντησις does not settle direction; Revelation 21 has the city descend and
God dwell with men; Matthew 5:5 promises the earth (or land); Romans 8:21
promises liberty to “the creation itself”; and Hebrews 11 says the
patriarchs saw the heavenly country *in* the promise of the land, not
instead of it. Hodge's “this renovated earth” and Poole's “the whole World
will then be the Possession, and Kingdom of the Saints” account for more
of the texts. Both Reformed views agree on what makes the place what it
is: “where Christ is” (Hodge), “*Vbi* Christ is” (Watson), ὅπου εἰμὶ ἐγώ
(John 14:3).

**Against the Jewish answers.** **[inferred]** The rabbinic hope of
inheriting the land, and of resurrection in it, is nearer to Hodge's
renewed earth than to Edwards's departure from “this accursed world”;
Hebrews 11 and Matthew 5:5 take up the same promise and widen it from the
land to the earth. Maimonides' bodiless world to come is further from the
Reformed than any rabbinic text gathered here: the Reformed agree with him
that the soul lives without the body before the resurrection, and differ
from him in making the raised body permanent. The Reform platforms keep
only the first half. See [jewish-sources.md](jewish-sources.md).
