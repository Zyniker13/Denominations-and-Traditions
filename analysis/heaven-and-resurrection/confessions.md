## Summary

1. **No confession numbers the heavens or mentions the “third heaven”.**
   The Westminster Standards and the Savoy Declaration say “the highest
   heavens”; the Second London Confession of 1689 replaces the phrase in
   32.1 (its 31.1) with “paradise, where they are with Christ”.
2. **All the Reformed confessions that treat the soul after death send it at
   once to Christ in heaven**, and the Westminster family adds that
   “Besides these two places for souls separated from their bodies, the
   Scripture acknowledgeth none.”
3. **All of them confess the resurrection of the same body**, in the
   strongest terms: “the self-same bodies, and none other” (Westminster),
   “their proper bodies, in which they formerly lived” (Belgic), “the
   substance of the self same flesh that every man now bears” (Scots).
4. **Only two speak of the world's end.** The Belgic Confession has Christ
   “burning this old world with fire and flame, to cleanse it”; the Scots
   Confession looks for “the time of refreshing and restitution of all
   things”. The Westminster Standards say nothing of the world's end.
5. **The final state is “heaven”** (Larger Catechism 90; Heidelberg 52,
   “into heavenly joys and glory”) or “everlasting life”, without a word on
   whether heaven will then be above the earth or on a renewed earth.
6. **The Lutheran confessions agree on the soul, the body and the judgment,
   and say little more.** Annihilation of the world is the teaching of the
   later dogmaticians, not of the Book of Concord. Where the Lutheran
   confessions go further it is on Christ's body: the right hand of God “is
   no fixed place in heaven”, and the Formula rejects the teaching that
   Christ's body “is so enclosed in heaven” that it cannot be present in the
   Supper.

## The ecumenical creeds

- **The Apostles' Creed**: “the resurrection of the body; and the life
  everlasting” (as printed in the Book of Concord and in the Heidelberg
  Catechism, Q. 22). Luther comments on the Latin *carnis resurrectionem*
  and the German *Auferstehung des Fleisches* in the Large Catechism (below).
- **The Nicene Creed**: “we look for the resurrection of the dead, and the
  life of the world to come.”
- **The Athanasian Creed**: “At whose coming all men will rise again with
  their bodies; And shall give account for their own works.”

## The Reformed confessions

### The heavens and the ascension

| Confession | Text |
| --- | --- |
| Westminster Confession 8.4 | Christ arose “with the same body in which he suffered; with which also he ascended into heaven, and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father”. |
| Westminster Larger Catechism 53 | Christ “visibly went up into the highest heavens, there to receive gifts for men, to raise up our affections thither, and to prepare a place for us, where himself is, and shall continue till his second coming at the end of the world.” |
| Heidelberg Catechism 46–49 | Christ “was taken up from earth into heaven”; “with respect to his human nature, he is no more on earth”; “we have our flesh in heaven as a sure pledge that he, as the head, will also take up to himself, us, his members”. Q76: “though Christ is in heaven and we on earth”. |
| Belgic Confession 19 | The human nature “remained a creature, having beginning of days, being a finite nature, and retaining all the properties of a real body. And though he hath by his resurrection given immortality to the same, nevertheless he hath not changed the reality of his human nature; forasmuch as our salvation and resurrection also depend on the reality of his body.” |
| Scots Confession 11 | “the same self body which was born of the virgin, was crucified, and buried, and which did rise again, did ascend into the heavens”. |
| Thirty-Nine Articles 4 | Christ “took again his body, with flesh, bones, and all things appertaining to the perfection of Man's nature; wherewith he ascended into Heaven, and there sitteth, until he return to judge all Men at the last day.” |

### The soul at death

| Confession | Text |
| --- | --- |
| Westminster Confession 32.1; Savoy 31.1 | The souls “(which neither die nor sleep)” of the righteous “are received into the highest heavens, where they behold the face of God in light and glory, waiting for the full redemption of their bodies … Besides these two places for souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none.” |
| Second London Confession 31.1 | “received into paradise, where they are with Christ, and behold the face of God in light and glory, waiting for the full redemption of their bodies”. |
| Westminster Larger Catechism 86 | “received into the highest heavens … waiting for the full redemption of their bodies, which even in death continue united to Christ, and rest in their graves as in their beds”. |
| Westminster Shorter Catechism 37 (Keach's Catechism 41) | “The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; and their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.” |
| Heidelberg Catechism 57 | “not only my soul after this life shall be immediately taken up to Christ its head”. |
| Scots Confession 17 | The elect departed are “in peace and rest from their labors, not that they will sleep and come to any certain oblivion (as some fanatics do affirm)”, citing the parable of Luke 16, “His words to the thief”, and the souls under the altar. |

### The resurrection of the body

| Confession | Text |
| --- | --- |
| Westminster Confession 32.2–3; Savoy 31.2–3; Second London 31.2–3 | “all the dead shall be raised up with the self-same bodies, and none other, although with different qualities, which shall be united again to their souls forever.” The bodies of the just are “made conformable to his own glorious body.” |
| Westminster Larger Catechism 87 | “the selfsame bodies of the dead which were laid in the grave … shall be raised up by the power of Christ. The bodies of the just … shall be raised in power, spiritual, incorruptible, and made like to his glorious body”. |
| Heidelberg Catechism 57 | “this my body, being raised by the power of Christ, shall be reunited with my soul, and made like unto the glorious body of Christ.” |
| Belgic Confession 37 | “all the dead shall be raised out of the earth, and their souls joined and united with their proper bodies, in which they formerly lived. As for those who shall then be living, they shall not die as the others, but be changed in the twinkling of an eye, and from corruptible, become incorruptible.” |
| Scots Confession 25 | “in the general judgment there will be given to every man and woman resurrection of the flesh … the dead will arise incorruptible and that in the substance of the self same flesh that every man now bears”, citing Isaiah 66, Daniel 12, 1 Corinthians 15 and Job 19. |

### The end of the world and the final state

| Confession | Text |
| --- | --- |
| Belgic Confession 37 | Christ will come “burning this old world with fire and flame, to cleanse it.” “the Lord will cause them to possess such a glory, as never entered into the heart of man to conceive.” |
| Scots Confession 11 | “the time of refreshing and restitution of all things will come, in so much that those who from the beginning have suffered violence, injury, and wrong for righteousness sake will inherit that blessed immortality promised from the beginning.” |
| Westminster Confession 33.2 | “then shall the righteous go into everlasting life, and receive that fulness of joy and refreshing which shall come from the presence of the Lord”. |
| Westminster Larger Catechism 90 | The righteous, “being caught up to Christ in the clouds … shall be received into heaven, where they shall be fully and forever freed from all sin and misery … made perfectly holy and happy both in body and soul, in the company of innumerable saints and holy angels, but especially in the immediate vision and fruition of God”. |
| Heidelberg Catechism 52, 58 | Christ “shall translate me with all his chosen ones to himself, into heavenly joys and glory”; “after this life, I shall inherit perfect salvation”. |

## The Lutheran confessions

- **Augsburg Confession XVII**: “at the Consummation of the World Christ will
  appear for judgment, and will raise up all the dead; He will give to the
  godly and elect eternal life and everlasting joys”. It condemns “others
  who are now spreading certain Jewish opinions, that before the
  resurrection of the dead the godly shall take possession of the kingdom of
  the world, the ungodly being everywhere suppressed.”
- **Apology XVII**: “The Seventeenth Article the adversaries receive without
  exception”, and repeats it: “at the consummation of the world Christ shall
  appear, and shall raise up all the dead”.
- **Small Catechism**, Creed III: the Holy Ghost “at the last day will raise
  up me and all the dead, and will give to me and to all believers in Christ
  everlasting life.”
- **Large Catechism**, Creed III, §§57–60: “we expect that our flesh will be
  destroyed and buried with all its uncleanness, and will come forth
  gloriously, and arise to entire and perfect holiness in a new eternal
  life.” “The term Auferstehung des Fleisches (resurrection of the flesh)
  here employed is not according to good German idiom … However, it is not a
  matter of much moment, if we only understand the words aright.”
- **Formula of Concord**, Solid Declaration I.46: “precisely the substance of
  this our flesh, but without sin, will rise again”; Epitome I.10, with Job
  19:26, “our nature which we now bear will rise and live eternally without
  original sin”.
- **On Christ's body and the right hand of God**: Solid Declaration VIII.28,
  the right hand of God “is no fixed place in heaven, as the Sacramentarians
  assert without any ground in the Holy Scriptures, but nothing else than the
  almighty power of God, which fills heaven and earth”; Epitome VII.14, that
  “God has and knows of various modes of being in any place, and not only the
  one … which philosophers call localis (local)”; and among the rejected
  teachings (Epitome VII, negative 11), “That the body of Christ is so
  enclosed in heaven that it can in no way be at once and at one time in
  many or all places upon earth where His Holy Supper is celebrated.”

The Smalcald Articles (I.4) repeat the creed, that Christ “will come to
judge the quick and the dead”; they, the Treatise and the Preface to the
Book of Concord say nothing more on these subjects. Among the Reformed, the
Canons of Dort speak of “eternal life” only as the gift of election.

## What the confessions leave open

**[inferred]**

- **The number of the heavens.** No confession counts them. The “third
  heaven” is the theologians' term (see [third-heaven.md](third-heaven.md)).
- **Where heaven is after the judgment.** The confessions say “heaven”,
  “heavenly joys”, “everlasting life”, and do not say whether the saints'
  home will then be above the world or on a renewed earth. The Belgic and
  Scots confessions, by “to cleanse it” and “restitution of all things”,
  lean toward a renewed world; the Westminster Standards do not address it.
  (See [final-destination.md](final-destination.md) and
  [new-heavens-and-new-earth.md](new-heavens-and-new-earth.md).)
- **What makes the raised body “the same”.** “Self-same”, “proper”, “the
  substance of the self same flesh” are confessed; how the identity is kept
  is not. Hodge's refusal to explain it is within the confessions; so is
  Boston's particle theory.
- **Annihilation or renewal.** The Lutheran confessions do not teach
  annihilation, and the Westminster Standards do not deny it. The Belgic
  Confession's “to cleanse it” sits ill with it: what is cleansed remains.
- **Where the Lutheran and Reformed confessions really differ** is not on
  the last things as such but on the body of Christ: the Heidelberg
  Catechism's “with respect to his human nature, he is no more on earth” and
  the Formula's rejection of a body “so enclosed in heaven”. That difference
  shapes how each tradition describes heaven and the glorified body (see
  [lutheran-and-reformed.md](lutheran-and-reformed.md)).
