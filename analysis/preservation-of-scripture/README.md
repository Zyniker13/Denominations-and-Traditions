# The Preservation of Scripture in Scripture and in the Confessions

What does Scripture itself claim about the endurance of God's word: that it
abides, does not pass away, does not fail, cannot be annulled, is not to be
altered, or is kept? How do those claims read in the Hebrew and in the Greek?
And do the confessions collected in this repository say more, less, or
something other than Scripture says?

**[context]** marks a statement resting on history outside the texts quoted;
**[inferred]** marks this study's own reading rather than what a text says in
so many words.

## Summary

1. **The claim Scripture makes most often is fulfilment.** In 21 passages
   God's word "will not fall", "will not return void", "will come to pass".
   What God says, he does. Next come permanence (12: the word "abides",
   "stands for ever", "shall not pass away") and the command to write (10).
2. **The strongest permanence claims are Isaiah 40:8 and Jesus's saying
   in Matthew 24:35.** "The word of our God abides for ever" (LXX τὸ δὲ ῥῆμα
   τοῦ θεοῦ ἡμῶν μένει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα; MT יָקוּם, "shall stand"); "heaven and
   earth will pass away, but my words will by no means pass away" (οἱ δὲ
   λόγοι μου οὐ μὴ παρέλθωσιν). 1 Peter 1:25 identifies the abiding word of
   Isaiah 40 with "the word which was preached to you".
3. **No passage says what form the enduring word will take.** None names a
   language, a text, a manuscript, or a line of copyists. The objects of
   the claims are God's word, words, counsel, commandments, law, truth,
   promise, and once "the scripture" (John 10:35).
4. **The passages that come closest to the text are about authority, not
   copying.** Matthew 5:18 and Luke 16:17 speak of the "iota" and "horn" of
   the law, and John 10:35 says "the scripture cannot be loosed". Read in
   the Greek, all three say that the law and Scripture cannot be annulled
   or lose their force, down to their least part (see
   [Inviolability](#inviolability)).
5. **The one Old Testament promise that something will be kept in its
   people's mouths through the generations is Isaiah 59:21**, and it is a
   promise of the Spirit and the words remaining in the mouths of the
   prophet's people. Psalm 12:7, often read of the words, is read of people
   by the Greek and is disputed in the Hebrew.
6. **Scripture shows God preserving his word through means**: commanding it
   to be written, deposited, sealed, committed to Israel, and rewritten after
   loss. Twice (the tables of Exodus 34, Jeremiah's scroll in Jeremiah 36)
   a destroyed text is restored by rewriting it.
7. **Scripture also admits that its word could be lost, falsified,
   perverted or annulled among men**: "the lying pen of the scribes"
   (Jeremiah 8:8), "ye have perverted the words of the living God" (23:36),
   "ye have made the word of God of none effect" (Matthew 15:6), those who
   "corrupt" or "twist" it (2 Corinthians 2:17; 2 Peter 3:16), a "famine of
   hearing the words of the LORD" (Amos 8:11). The commands not to add or
   take away presuppose that men can do both.
8. **[inferred] Overall**, Scripture strongly and repeatedly claims that
   God's word is permanent, will be fulfilled, cannot be annulled, and will
   remain with his people. It does not claim that any written copy of it
   will be kept free of error, and it records the opposite happening in its
   own history. A doctrine that God preserves his word, its message and
   authority, among his people is what the passages state. A doctrine that
   he preserves a particular text without error is an inference from them,
   not something any of them says.

## Scope and method

### What was collected

A passage was included if it makes a claim, gives a command, or narrates an
event about the endurance, reliability or keeping of God's word. The
question asked was about God "making a claim concerning His Word enduring,
not passing away, not being corrupted, et cetera", so the core of the
collection is the passages in which God or Jesus speaks. Around that core
the collection includes:

- passages in which a prophet, psalmist or apostle makes the same kind of
  claim in his own voice. They are part of what the confessions mean by
  "Scripture" and are cited by them;
- passages that show *how* the word was kept: writing, deposit, custody,
  restoration;
- passages that admit the word's corruption, nullification or withdrawal
  among men, since a claim of preservation has to be read alongside them.

### Who is speaking

Each passage is tagged with its speaker:

| Speaker | Passages | Tier |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| God, directly | 5 | God speaking (38) |
| God, through a prophet ("thus saith the LORD") | 17 | |
| Jesus | 12 | |
| A heavenly voice (Isaiah 40:6; an angel in Daniel 12, Luke 1, Revelation 22) | 4 | |
| Moses or a prophet speaking for God in his own voice | 6 | For God (6) |
| A psalmist | 13 | About God's word (33) |
| An apostle | 14 | |
| A narrator | 2 | |
| A sage (Agur, Proverbs 30) | 1 | |
| Another speaker (Solomon, Jehu, Stephen) | 3 | |

The tier matters for weighing a claim: what God says of his own word is the
strongest kind of claim; what a psalmist confesses about it is testimony.
**[inferred]** On the confessions' own doctrine every passage of Scripture is
God's word, so all three tiers count as Scripture's claims; the tiers are
given so that a reader who distinguishes them can.

### What role the passage plays

| Role | Passages | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| claim | 50 | Asserts something about the word's endurance, reliability or inviolability |
| means | 14 | Describes how it is kept: writing, deposit, custody, restoration, the Spirit's recall |
| warning | 6 | Admits or threatens its corruption, perversion, nullification or withdrawal |
| command | 5 | Forbids altering it, or commands that it be kept |
| ground | 2 | The attribute of God on which the other claims rest: he does not lie |

### Texts

The Old Testament passages are given in the Hebrew of the Westminster
Leningrad Codex and the Greek of Brenton's Septuagint; the New Testament in
the Textus Receptus, the Byzantine text of 1904, and the SBL Greek New
Testament. The analysis works from the Hebrew and Greek; the KJV and Brenton
are printed as translations. Details are in
[scripture/preservation/README.md](../../scripture/preservation/README.md#editions).

Two things stand out. God's own claims about his word are overwhelmingly
claims about its *fulfilment* and *permanence*, not its transcription. And
the same voice that promises the word's endurance also commands it to be
written, rewrites it when it is destroyed, forbids its alteration, and
charges men with having falsified it.

## Analysis by category

### Fulfilment

[Numbers 23:19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#num-23-19);
[Joshua 21:45; 23:14](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#josh-21-45);

[45:23](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-45-23);
[46:10–11](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-46-10);
[55:10–11](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-55-11);
[Jeremiah 1:12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-1-12);
[Ezekiel 12:25, 28](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ezek-12-25);
[Habakkuk 2:2–3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#hab-2-2);
[Zechariah 1:5–6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#zech-1-5);
[Matthew 5:17–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-5-18);
[Luke 1:37](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#luke-1-37);
[24:44](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#luke-24-44);
[Romans 9:6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rom-9-6);
[Hebrews 6:17–18](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#heb-6-18).

The Old Testament's characteristic idiom is that God's word does not "fall"
(Hebrew נָפַל; Greek πίπτω, Luke 16:17 πεσεῖν, Romans 9:6 ἐκπέπτωκεν) and does
not "return" to him empty (שׁוּב; ἀποστρέφω). It is said of promises and
threats, and is proved by events: "there failed not ought of any good thing
which the LORD had spoken; all came to pass" (Joshua 21:45). The ground is
God's character: he is "not a man, that he should lie" (Numbers 23:19), and
"it was impossible for God to lie" (ἀδύνατον ψεύσασθαι θεόν, Hebrews 6:18).

**Assessment.** This is the most frequent and the most consistent claim in
the collection, in both Testaments and in both the Hebrew and the Greek. Its
object is the *content* of what God says, promises and threatens, and its
evidence is history. It is a claim about God's reliability, and only
indirectly about a text: a promise has to be known to be seen to be kept.

### Permanence

[Psalm 19:9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-19-9);
[33:11](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-33-11);
[105:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-105-8);
[111:7–8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-111-7);
[117:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-117-2);
[119:89–91](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-119-89);
[119:152, 160](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-119-152);
[Isaiah 40:6–8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-40-8);
[Zechariah 1:5–6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#zech-1-5);
[Matthew 24:35](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-24-35);
[John 12:48](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-12-48);
[1 Peter 1:23–25](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1pet-1-23).

The Psalms say God's counsel, commandments, testimonies, judgments and truth
stand or endure "for ever" (לְעוֹלָם, לָעַד; εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα). Psalm 119:89 places
the word "settled in heaven". Isaiah 40:8 contrasts it with mortal flesh,
and 1 Peter 1:23–25 takes Isaiah's Greek wording over (μένει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα)
and adds: "this is the word which by the gospel is preached unto you" (τοῦτο
δέ ἐστιν τὸ ῥῆμα τὸ εὐαγγελισθὲν εἰς ὑμᾶς). Jesus's saying goes furthest:
his words outlast heaven and earth (Matthew 24:35), and the word he has
spoken will judge at the last day (John 12:48).

**Assessment.** The permanence passages say that God's word does not
expire: it remains true, valid and in force for ever. Psalm 119:89 locates
its permanence in heaven, and 1 Peter locates the abiding word in the
gospel preached. **[inferred]** None of them says the word abides *in a
book*; the nearest, 1 Peter 1:25, says it abides as the message preached.
The Greek makes the theme uniform: four Hebrew expressions become μένει or
διαμένει (see [greek-consistency.md §1](greek-consistency.md#1-abides-for-ever-μένω-and-διαμένω)).

### Inviolability

[Matthew 5:17–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-5-18);
[Luke 16:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#luke-16-17);
[John 10:35](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-10-35).

These three are the only passages that speak of the parts of the law or of
"the scripture" as such, and they are therefore the ones most often read as
promises about the text.

- **Matthew 5:18.** "Until heaven and earth pass away, one iota or one horn
  shall by no means pass away from the law, until all things take place"
  (ἰῶτα ἓν ἢ μία κεραία οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἀπὸ τοῦ νόμου). In the Greek, ἰῶτα is
  the smallest letter of the Greek alphabet and κεραία ("horn") the least
  stroke of a letter; together they are an idiom for the least particle. The
  verb is the one used of heaven and earth in the same sentence, "pass away",
  cease to stand. Verse 19 draws the consequence with "therefore" (οὖν):
  whoever "loosens" (λύσῃ) one of the least commandments and teaches others
  so. What does not pass away is the binding force of the law down to its
  least commandment. The claim is also bounded: "until heaven and earth pass
  away" and "until all things take place". The full reading is in the
  companion study,
  [§1.1, "Matthew 5:18 read in the Greek"](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#11-matthew-518-read-in-the-greek).
- **Luke 16:17.** "It is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for
  one horn of the law to fall" (μίαν κεραίαν πεσεῖν). "Fall" is the idiom of
  the fulfilment passages for a word that fails of its effect.
- **John 10:35.** "The scripture cannot be loosed" (οὐ δύναται λυθῆναι ἡ
  γραφή): said of a single phrase of Psalm 82:6, which Jesus treats as
  binding because it is written. λύω is the verb of Matthew 5:19.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Read in the Greek, all three are claims about
authority: the law and Scripture cannot be annulled, down to their least
part. That is a strong claim, and it reaches the smallest detail; but its
verbs (παρέρχομαι, πίπτω, λύω) are verbs of validity, not of copying, and no
one of the three mentions scribes, copies or a language. They presuppose
that the law and Scripture remain knowable, since an annulled commandment
must be one that people know, but they do not say how. They are the passages
the confessions cite for the purity of the text (see
[confessions.md](confessions.md)).

### Inscripturation

[Exodus 17:14](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#exod-17-14);
[34:1](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#exod-34-1);
[Deuteronomy 31:24–26](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-31-24);
[Psalm 102:18](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-102-18);
[Isaiah 30:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-30-8);
[34:16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-34-16);
[Jeremiah 30:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-30-2);
[36](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-36);
[Habakkuk 2:2–3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#hab-2-2);
[Revelation 1:19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-1-19).

God repeatedly commands that his word be written, and gives the reason:
"for a memorial" (Exodus 17:14), "for the time to come for ever and ever"
(Isaiah 30:8, as the Masoretes pointed it), "for the generation to come"
(Psalm 102:18), "that he may run that readeth it" (Habakkuk 2:2).

**Assessment.** Writing is the means Scripture itself gives for the word
reaching later generations. **[inferred]** This is the strongest scriptural
basis for the confessions' statement that God committed his word to
writing "for the better preserving and propagating of the truth" (WCF 1.1;
Belgic 3); the passages support it well.

### Transmission

[Deuteronomy 18:18–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-18-18);
[29:29](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-29-29);
[Joshua 1:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#josh-1-8);
[Psalm 12:6–7](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-12-6);
[Isaiah 59:21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-59-21);
[John 14:26; 16:13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-14-26);
[Acts 7:38](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#acts-7-38);
[Romans 15:4](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rom-15-4);
[Jude 3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jude-3).

- **Isaiah 59:21** is the only passage in which God promises that his words
  will remain with a people across generations: "my words which I have put
  in thy mouth shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of
  thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed, from henceforth and for
  ever." The promise is of the Spirit and the words together, in the mouth.
  The Greek lacks "thy seed's seed" (see
  [hebrew-and-greek.md](hebrew-and-greek.md#differences-that-change-the-claim)).
- **Psalm 12:7** is the passage most often cited for the preservation of the
  words. In the Hebrew, "thou shalt keep *them*" (תִּשְׁמְרֵם) is followed by
  "thou shalt preserve *him*" (תִּצְּרֶנּוּ), and the nearest plural antecedent
  is the poor and needy of verse 5. The Greek reads "thou shalt keep *us*,
  and preserve *us*" (φυλάξεις ἡμᾶς καὶ διατηρήσεις ἡμᾶς). **[inferred]** The
  reading "the words" is possible in the Hebrew but not required, and is
  excluded by the Greek.
- **John 14:26** promises the apostles that the Spirit will "bring to your
  remembrance all that I said to you" (ὑπομνήσει ὑμᾶς πάντα ἃ εἶπον ὑμῖν): a
  promise about the apostles' memory, the means by which Jesus's words
  reached writing.
- **Romans 15:4** and **Deuteronomy 29:29** say the word was given "for our
  learning" and belongs "to us and to our children for ever", which
  presupposes that it would reach them.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Scripture promises that God's word will
remain with his people. Where it says how, it names the Spirit, the mouth,
memory and writing. It does not say that the written form will be kept
without variation.

### Purity and truth

[2 Samuel 22:31; Psalm 18:30](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2sam-22-31);
[Psalm 12:6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-12-6);
[19:9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-19-9);
[119:140](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-119-140);
[Proverbs 30:5](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#prov-30-5);
[John 17:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-17-17);
[1 Peter 2:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1pet-2-2);
[Revelation 22:6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-22-6).

The Old Testament image is metallurgical: God's word is "refined"
(צְרוּפָה; πεπυρωμένον, "tried in the fire"), like "silver tried in a furnace,
purified seven times" (Psalm 12:6). **[inferred]** The image is of something
without dross, tested and found true: it describes the word as God speaks
it, not the state of copies. The New Testament drops the image and speaks
of truth (ἀλήθεια, John 17:17; ἀληθινοί, Revelation 22:6) and of "the
unadulterated milk of the word" (τὸ λογικὸν ἄδολον γάλα, 1 Peter 2:2),
ἄδολος being the word for goods not cut with anything else (see
[greek-consistency.md §7](greek-consistency.md#7-pure-tried-πυρόω-and-ἁγνός-and-what-replaces-them)).

**Assessment.** These are claims about the word's quality: it is true, and
it contains nothing false. They are the proper scriptural ground for the
"purity" of Scripture as a quality of God's word (as in WLC Q4). They are not
claims about textual transmission.

### Efficacy

[Deuteronomy 8:3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-8-3);
[Isaiah 55:10–11](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-55-11);
[Jeremiah 23:29](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-23-28);
[Matthew 4:4](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-4-4);
[Acts 7:38](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#acts-7-38);
[1 Thessalonians 2:13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1thess-2-13);
[2 Timothy 2:9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2tim-2-9);
[Hebrews 4:12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#heb-4-12).

The word is "living" (ζῶν, Hebrews 4:12; λόγια ζῶντα, Acts 7:38), "works"
in believers (ἐνεργεῖται, 1 Thessalonians 2:13), is "not bound" (οὐ δέδεται,
2 Timothy 2:9) though its preacher is, and is "like a fire" and "a hammer"
(Jeremiah 23:29). **Assessment.** These passages make the word's endurance
active: it is not only kept, it does what God sends it to do. That is the
reading of preservation the Lutheran confessions favour (see
[confessions.md](confessions.md#book-of-concord)).

### Prohibition of alteration

[Deuteronomy 4:2; 12:32](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-4-2);
[Proverbs 30:6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#prov-30-5);
[Jeremiah 26:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-26-2);
[Revelation 22:18–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-22-18).

"Ye shall not add unto the word … neither shall ye diminish ought from it"
(Deuteronomy 4:2; οὐ προσθήσετε … οὐκ ἀφελεῖτε). Revelation 22:18–19 attaches
the plagues of the book to adding and the loss of one's part to taking away.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** These are commands to people, with sanctions.
A prohibition presupposes that the forbidden act is possible; so these
passages, more than any others, show that Scripture did *not* regard the
alteration of God's words by men as impossible. Deuteronomy's command
concerns the commandments Moses was giving (what Israel was to do);
Revelation's concerns "the words of the prophecy of this book". Neither is a
promise that alteration will not happen.

### Custody

[Deuteronomy 31:24–26](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-31-24);
[Isaiah 8:16, 20](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-8-16);
[Daniel 12:4, 9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#dan-12-4);
[Romans 3:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rom-3-2).

The book of the law is laid beside the ark "for a witness" (Deuteronomy
31:26); the testimony is sealed "among my disciples" (Isaiah 8:16, Hebrew
only); Daniel's book is sealed "to the time of the end"; and to the Jews "were
committed the oracles of God" (ἐπιστεύθησαν τὰ λόγια τοῦ θεοῦ, Romans 3:2).

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Scripture assigns the written word to human
keepers: priests, disciples, a nation. Romans 3:2 says the oracles were
*entrusted* (πιστεύω), a word that implies a duty of faithful keeping, not a
guarantee of it; the next verse asks "what if some were unfaithful?"
(ἠπίστησαν). Romans 3:2 is the text the Helvetic Consensus (Canon II) and
the Second London Confession (1.8) cite for the Hebrew text.

### Restoration

[Exodus 34:1; Deuteronomy 10:2, 4](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#exod-34-1);
[Jeremiah 36](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-36).

The first tables are broken and God writes the same words again "according
to the first writing" (Deuteronomy 10:4). Jehoiakim burns Jeremiah's scroll
column by column, and God commands that "all the former words" be written
again; "and there were added besides unto them many like words" (Jeremiah
36:32).

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** These are the only narratives in Scripture
of a written word of God being destroyed, and in both God's answer is to
have it written again. In the second, the restored text is *longer* than the
lost one. Scripture's own model of preservation here is not an unbroken
chain of copies but renewed writing after loss.

### Corruption and withdrawal

[Jeremiah 8:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-8-8);
[23:28–29, 36](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-23-28);
[Matthew 15:6; Mark 7:13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-15-6);
[2 Corinthians 2:17; 4:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2cor-2-17);
[2 Peter 3:16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2pet-3-16);
[Amos 8:11–12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#amos-8-11).

- **Jeremiah 8:8**: "How do ye say, We are wise, and the law of the LORD is
  with us? Lo, certainly in vain made he it; the pen of the scribes is in
  vain" (KJV). The Hebrew is sharper: "the lying pen of the scribes has made
  it into a lie" (לַשֶּׁקֶר עָשָׂה עֵט שֶׁקֶר סֹפְרִים). **[inferred]** Whether
  this charges the scribes with miscopying the law or with false teaching
  in writing is disputed; either way, God says that what scribes wrote
  under the name of his law was false.
- **Jeremiah 23:36**: "ye have perverted (הֲפַכְתֶּם) the words of the living
  God" (Hebrew only).
- **Matthew 15:6**: "ye have made the word of God of none effect
  (ἠκυρώσατε) by your tradition". The text stands; its force is cancelled.
- **2 Corinthians 2:17**: "we are not as many, which corrupt (καπηλεύοντες,
  peddle, adulterate as a huckster waters wine) the word of God"; 4:2,
  "nor handling the word of God deceitfully" (δολοῦντες).
- **2 Peter 3:16**: the unlearned "wrest" (στρεβλοῦσιν, twist) Paul's letters
  "as they do also the other scriptures".
- **Amos 8:11**: God will send "a famine of hearing the words of the LORD;
  they shall seek the word of the LORD, and shall not find it."

**Assessment.** Scripture does not claim that God's word is safe from human
corruption; it repeatedly says the opposite. The corruptions it names are of
teaching, interpretation and practice more than of copies (Jeremiah 8:8 is
the one that may be about writing), and its remedy is the prophet's and the
apostle's faithful word, not a guarantee about manuscripts. Amos adds that
God may judge a people by withdrawing his word from them. **[inferred]** A
doctrine of preservation consistent with Scripture has to allow for both.

### Origin

[2 Timothy 3:15–16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2tim-3-16);
[2 Peter 1:19–21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2pet-1-19).

"All scripture is God-breathed" (πᾶσα γραφὴ θεόπνευστος); prophecy came not
by the will of man but as men "were borne along by the Holy Spirit"
(φερόμενοι). **Assessment.** These are claims about where Scripture comes
from, not how it is kept. They are included because the confessions join
inspiration and preservation in one sentence ("immediately inspired by God,
and by his singular care and providence kept pure", WCF 1.8). **[inferred]**
2 Timothy 3:15–16 does show that Paul regarded the Scriptures Timothy had
known "from a child", copies in use in the first century, and very likely
the Greek ones, as "God-breathed" and able to make him wise to salvation.

### Exaltation

[Psalm 138:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-138-2). "Thou hast
magnified thy word above all thy name" (Hebrew); "thou hast magnified thy
holy name above every thing" (Greek). The exaltation of the word is in the
Hebrew only.

## What the passages do not say

**[inferred]** Taking the 77 passages together:

1. **No passage names a language, text or manuscript tradition** as the form
   in which God's word will endure. The question "preserved in the Hebrew or
   in the Greek?" is not answered by any of them; it is the subject of the
   companion study
   [preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md).
2. **No passage promises that copies of Scripture will be free of error.**
   The nearest candidates are Matthew 5:18, Luke 16:17 and John 10:35, which
   are about authority; and Psalm 12:7, whose object is disputed in the
   Hebrew and is "us" in the Greek.
3. **No Greek verb in either Testament has God "keeping" a text.** God keeps
   people (φυλάσσω, τηρέω), and people keep the word (see
   [greek-consistency.md §8](greek-consistency.md#8-keep-preserve-φυλάσσω-and-τηρέω)).
4. **Several passages show the means by which God kept his word**: writing,
   deposit, a people entrusted with it, the Spirit's work in memory and in
   the mouth, rewriting after loss.
5. **Several passages admit corruption among men**, and the commands not to
   add or take away presuppose it is possible.

## Assessment

**[inferred]** Three readings of a doctrine of preservation are possible
(the companion study sets them out in
[§8](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#8-what-this-means-for-the-preservation-claims)):

| Reading | What it holds | How the passages bear on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Message and efficacy** | God's word, its content and power, abides and accomplishes its purpose among his people | **Stated directly** and often: fulfilment, permanence, efficacy, transmission, Isaiah 59:21, 1 Peter 1:25 |
| **Providential sufficiency** | God by his providence keeps the Scriptures in a state sufficient for faith and life, through means and despite errors in copies | **Consonant**: inscripturation, custody, restoration and the inviolability sayings fit it; no passage states it in those words, and none contradicts it |
| **Verbal preservation** | God keeps a particular text, to its letters, free from error in every age | **Not stated by any passage.** It can be inferred only by reading Matthew 5:18 or Psalm 12:7 as promises about copies, which their Greek (and, for Psalm 12, the Greek translation) does not support; and it sits uneasily with the passages that admit corruption and record loss and rewriting |

Scripture's own claim is therefore strong in one direction and silent in
another. It claims, more often and more emphatically than almost anything
else it says about itself, that God's word will not fail, pass away or be
annulled. It does not claim to know which copies will carry it, and it
expects the people who carry it to be capable of falsifying it. How the
confessions handle that gap is the subject of [confessions.md](confessions.md).
