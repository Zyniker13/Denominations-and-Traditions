# The Preservation of Scripture in Scripture and in the Confessions

What does Scripture itself claim about the endurance of God's word: that it
abides, does not pass away, does not fail, cannot be annulled, is not to be
altered, or is kept? How do those claims read in the Hebrew and in the Greek?
And do the confessions collected in this repository say more, less, or
something other than Scripture says?

**[context]** marks a statement resting on history outside the texts quoted;
**[inferred]** marks this study's own reading rather than what a text says in
so many words.

## Summary

1. **The claim Scripture makes most often is fulfilment.** In 35 passages
   God's word "will not fall", "will not return void", "will come to pass",
   "must be fulfilled". What God says, he does. Next come transmission to
   later generations (33), the writing of the word (29), and permanence (15:
   the word "abides", "stands for ever", "shall not pass away").
2. **The strongest permanence claims are Isaiah 40:8 and Jesus's saying
   in Matthew 24:35.** "The word of our God abides for ever" (LXX τὸ δὲ ῥῆμα
   τοῦ θεοῦ ἡμῶν μένει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα; MT יָקוּם, "shall stand"); "heaven and
   earth will pass away, but my words will by no means pass away" (οἱ δὲ
   λόγοι μου οὐ μὴ παρέλθωσιν). 1 Peter 1:25 identifies the abiding word of
   Isaiah 40 with "the word which was preached to you".
3. **No passage promises a particular text or line of copyists.** The
   objects of the claims are God's word, words, counsel, commandments, law,
   truth, promise, and once "the scripture" (John 10:35). The one passage
   that measures the word by its letters, Matthew 5:18, measures it in a
   Greek letter, the iota, which counts for the Greek.
4. **The passages that come closest to the text are about authority, not
   copying.** Matthew 5:18 and Luke 16:17 speak of the "iota" and "horn" of
   the law, and John 10:35 says "the scripture cannot be loosed". Read in
   the Greek, all three say that the law and Scripture cannot be annulled
   or lose their force, down to their least part (see
   [Inviolability](#inviolability)).
5. **Two Old Testament passages promise that God's words will stay in
   the mouths of later generations.** Isaiah 59:21 promises the Spirit and
   the words in the mouth of the prophet's seed "for ever"; Deuteronomy
   31:21 promises that the Song of Moses "shall not be forgotten out of the
   mouths of their seed", as a witness against them. Psalm 12:7, often read
   of the words, is read of people by the Greek and is disputed in the
   Hebrew.
6. **Scripture shows God preserving his word through means**: commanding it
   to be written, copied (the king's copy, Deuteronomy 17:18), deposited,
   read aloud at set times, taught to children, sealed, committed to Israel,
   and rewritten after loss. Twice (the tables of Exodus 34, Jeremiah's
   scroll in Jeremiah 36) a destroyed text is restored by rewriting it, and
   once (2 Kings 22) a neglected book of the law is found in the temple. In
   the New Testament the means are the apostles' handing on (παράδοσις,
   παραθήκη) to faithful men, letters read in the churches, and the
   believers' "keeping" of the word.
7. **Scripture also admits that its word could be lost, falsified,
   perverted or annulled among men**: "the lying pen of the scribes"
   (Jeremiah 8:8), "ye have perverted the words of the living God" (23:36),
   "ye have made the word of God of none effect" (Matthew 15:6), those who
   "corrupt" or "twist" it (2 Corinthians 2:17; 2 Peter 3:16), a "famine of
   hearing the words of the LORD" (Amos 8:11), a letter forged "as from us"
   (2 Thessalonians 2:2). It cites prophetic writings that no longer exist
   (2 Chronicles 9:29) and an apostolic letter that is not extant (1
   Corinthians 5:9). The commands not to add or take away presuppose that
   men can do both.
8. **[inferred] Overall**, Scripture strongly and repeatedly claims that
   God's word is permanent, will be fulfilled, cannot be annulled, and will
   remain with his people. It does not claim that any written copy of it
   will be kept free of error, and it records the opposite happening in its
   own history. A doctrine that God preserves his word, its message and
   authority, among his people is what the passages state. A doctrine that
   he preserves a particular text without error is an inference from them,
   not something any of them says.

## Scope and method

### What was collected

A passage was included if it makes a claim, gives a command, or narrates an
event about the endurance, reliability or keeping of God's word, even if it
does so only in passing. The
question asked was about God "making a claim concerning His Word enduring,
not passing away, not being corrupted, et cetera", so the core of the
collection is the passages in which God or Jesus speaks. Around that core
the collection includes:

- passages in which a prophet, psalmist or apostle makes the same kind of
  claim in his own voice. They are part of what the confessions mean by
  "Scripture" and are cited by them;
- passages that show *how* the word was kept: writing, deposit, custody,
  restoration;
- passages that admit the word's corruption, nullification or withdrawal
  among men, or record writings that were lost, since a claim of
  preservation has to be read alongside them;
- passages in which the faithful are said to keep, hold fast, or abide in
  the word, the human side of its keeping.

The collection was first made from the passages commonly cited on the
subject, then checked by a systematic search of the Hebrew and Greek texts
of both Testaments (not the English) for the vocabulary of the theme: in the
Hebrew, for example, דָּבָר and אִמְרָה with קוּם, עָמַד, נָפַל, שׁוּב, לְעוֹלָם,
and כָּתַב, סֵפֶר, מִשְׁנֶה, שָׁמַר, נָצַר, שָׁכַח; in the Greek, λόγος, ῥῆμα,
γραφή and γράμμα with μένω, παρέρχομαι, πληρόω, τελέω, λύω, ἀκυρόω, ἀθετέω,
τηρέω, φυλάσσω, κατέχω, κρατέω, παραδίδωμι and παραθήκη. Each hit was read in
context. What was then left out, and why, is at the
[end of this file](#passages-considered-and-left-out).

given so that a reader who distinguishes them can.

forbids its alteration, and charges men, including its appointed keepers,
with having falsified it.

The Old Testament's characteristic idiom is that God's word does not "fall"
(Hebrew נָפַל; Greek πίπτω, Luke 16:17 πεσεῖν, Romans 9:6 ἐκπέπτωκεν) and does
not "return" to him empty (שׁוּב; ἀποστρέφω). It is said of promises and
threats, and is proved by events: "there failed not ought of any good thing
which the LORD had spoken; all came to pass" (Joshua 21:45). The ground is
God's character: he is "not a man, that he should lie" (Numbers 23:19), and
"it was impossible for God to lie" (ἀδύνατον ψεύσασθαι θεόν, Hebrews 6:18).

The same claim is made in prayer and praise: David asks God to "establish
for ever" the word spoken to him and confesses "thy words be true" (2 Samuel
7:25, 28); Solomon says God "spake with thy mouth, and hast fulfilled it with
thine hand" (1 Kings 8:24); the Levites, "thou hast performed thy words"
(Nehemiah 9:8); Lamentations, that God "hath fulfilled his word that he had
commanded in the days of old" (2:17), a threat, not a promise. Deuteronomy
18:22 and Jeremiah 28:9 turn it into a test: a word that does not come to
pass is not God's. Jeremiah 44:28 sets God's words and men's against each
other: "they shall know whose words shall stand, mine, or theirs."

In the New Testament the claim takes the form of necessity. Jesus says the
Scriptures "must" be fulfilled (δεῖ, Matthew 26:54; Luke 22:37; 24:44),
Peter that "this scripture must needs have been fulfilled, which the Holy
Ghost by the mouth of David spake" (Acts 1:16), Paul that "all the promises
of God in him are yea" (2 Corinthians 1:20), and the angel of Revelation
that the mystery will be finished "as he hath declared to his servants the
prophets" (10:7). The many single fulfilment notices of the Gospels ("that
it might be fulfilled which was spoken …") are not listed one by one; the
passages listed are those that state the principle.

**Assessment.** This is the most frequent and the most consistent claim in
the collection, in both Testaments and in both the Hebrew and the Greek. Its
object is the *content* of what God says, promises and threatens, and its
evidence is history. It is a claim about God's reliability, and only
indirectly about a text: a promise has to be known to be seen to be kept.

The Psalms say God's counsel, commandments, testimonies, judgments and truth
stand or endure "for ever" (לְעוֹלָם, לָעַד; εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα). Psalm 119:89 places
the word "settled in heaven". Isaiah 40:8 contrasts it with mortal flesh,
and 1 Peter 1:23–25 takes Isaiah's Greek wording over (μένει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα)
and adds: "this is the word which by the gospel is preached unto you" (τοῦτο
δέ ἐστιν τὸ ῥῆμα τὸ εὐαγγελισθὲν εἰς ὑμᾶς). Jesus's saying goes furthest:
his words outlast heaven and earth (Matthew 24:35), and the word he has
spoken will judge at the last day (John 12:48). Revelation 14:6 calls the
gospel "everlasting" (εὐαγγέλιον αἰώνιον), and 1 John 2:14, 24 and 2 John 2
place the abiding (μένω) in believers: "the word of God abideth in you";
"the truth … which dwelleth in us, and shall be with us for ever".

**Assessment.** The permanence passages say that God's word does not
expire: it remains true, valid and in force for ever. Psalm 119:89 locates
its permanence in heaven, and 1 Peter locates the abiding word in the
gospel preached. **[inferred]** None of them says the word abides *in a
book*; the nearest, 1 Peter 1:25, says it abides as the message preached,
and 1 John and 2 John say it abides in those who believe it.
The Greek makes the theme uniform: four Hebrew expressions become μένει or
διαμένει (see [greek-consistency.md §1](greek-consistency.md#1-abides-for-ever-μένω-and-διαμένω)).

### Inviolability

[Matthew 5:17–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-5-18);
[Luke 16:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#luke-16-17);
[John 10:35; 7:23](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-10-35);
[Hebrews 2:1–4](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#heb-2-2);
[Galatians 3:15, 17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#gal-3-15).

The first three are the only passages that speak of the parts of the law or
of "the scripture" as such, and they are therefore the ones most often read
as promises about the text. Two passages from the apostles use the same
vocabulary: Galatians 3:15, 17 (a ratified covenant no one "annuls" or "adds
to", ἀθετεῖ, ἐπιδιατάσσεται; the law "cannot disannul", ἀκυροῖ, the promise)
and Hebrews 2:2 (the word spoken through angels "was stedfast", βέβαιος).

- **Matthew 5:18.** "Until heaven and earth pass away, one iota or one horn
  shall by no means pass away from the law, until all things take place"
  (ἰῶτα ἓν ἢ μία κεραία οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἀπὸ τοῦ νόμου). ἰῶτα is the smallest
  letter of the Greek alphabet and κεραία ("horn") the least stroke of a
  letter: not the least letter or stroke of the Law. The text measures the
  Law in Greek letters, and the Evangelist, who writes Hebrew words in Greek
  letters when he means them (ἀμήν in the same verse), wrote ἰῶτα, not ἰώδ.
  Taking the Greek as the text, the verse speaks of a Law written in Greek
  letters, which counts for the Greek. The
  verb is the one used of heaven and earth in the same sentence, "pass away",
  cease to stand. Verse 19 draws the consequence with "therefore" (οὖν):
  whoever "loosens" (λύσῃ) one of the least commandments and teaches others
  so. What does not pass away is the binding force of the law down to its
  least commandment. The claim is also bounded: "until heaven and earth pass
  away" and "until all things take place". The full reading is in the
  companion study,
  [§1.1, "Matthew 5:18 read in the Greek"](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#11-matthew-518-read-in-the-greek).
- **Luke 16:17.** "It is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for
  one horn of the law to fall" (μίαν κεραίαν πεσεῖν). "Fall" is the idiom of
  the fulfilment passages for a word that fails of its effect.
- **John 10:35.** "The scripture cannot be loosed" (οὐ δύναται λυθῆναι ἡ
  γραφή): said of a single phrase of Psalm 82:6, which Jesus treats as
  binding because it is written. λύω is the verb of Matthew 5:19, and of
  John 7:23 ("that the law of Moses should not be broken").

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Read in the Greek, all five are claims about
authority: the law and Scripture cannot be annulled, down to their least
part. That is a strong claim, and it reaches the smallest detail; but its
verbs (παρέρχομαι, πίπτω, λύω, ἀθετέω, ἀκυρόω) are verbs of validity, not of
copying, and none of the five mentions scribes or copies. Matthew 5:18 does name a
letter, and the letter is Greek. They presuppose
that the law and Scripture remain knowable, since an annulled commandment
must be one that people know, but they do not say how. They are the passages
the confessions cite for the purity of the text (see
[confessions.md](confessions.md)).

[2 Peter 1:12–15; 3:1–2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2pet-1-12).

God repeatedly commands that his word be written, and gives the reason:
"for a memorial" (Exodus 17:14), "for the time to come for ever and ever"
(Isaiah 30:8, as the Masoretes pointed it), "for the generation to come"
(Psalm 102:18), "that he may run that readeth it" (Habakkuk 2:2).

At Sinai the word is written twice over: God writes the tables "with the
finger of God", "the writing of God" (ἡ γραφὴ γραφὴ Θεοῦ, Exodus 31:18;
32:16), and Moses writes "all the words of the LORD" in "the book of the
covenant" (24:4, 7). The law is then to be written on doorposts (Deuteronomy
6:9), on plastered stones "very plainly" (27:8; Joshua 8:32), and in a
**copy** made by the king "out of that which is before the priests the
Levites" (Deuteronomy 17:18). This last is the only command in Scripture to
copy the law; the Hebrew calls the copy מִשְׁנֵה, "a second", and the Greek
names the whole book from it, τὸ δευτερονόμιον. The book could receive
further writing: Joshua writes "in the book of the law of God" (Joshua
24:26), Samuel lays his book "up before the LORD" (1 Samuel 10:25), and
Jeremiah's restored scroll has "many like words" added (36:32). God himself
is said to write (Hosea 8:12, "I have written to him the great things of my
law"), and to hand Ezekiel his message already written (Ezekiel 2:9–3:3).

The sages give the purpose of writing: "Have not I written to thee …, that
I might make thee know the certainty of the words of truth?" (Proverbs
22:19–21, Hebrew; the Greek instead tells the reader to copy them out,
"write them for thyself threefold"), and "that which was written was
upright, even words of truth … given from one shepherd" (Ecclesiastes
12:10–11). The New Testament writers say the same of their own books: Luke
writes "that thou mightest know the certainty (ἀσφάλειαν)" (1:4), John
"that ye might believe" (20:31), and Peter so that his readers can "have
these things always in remembrance" after his death (2 Peter 1:15). Jesus
sets Moses's "writings" beside his own words (John 5:47).

**Assessment.** Writing is the means Scripture itself gives for the word
reaching later generations. **[inferred]** This is the strongest scriptural
basis for the confessions' statement that God committed his word to
writing "for the better preserving and propagating of the truth" (WCF 1.1;
Belgic 3); the passages support it well, and WCF 1.1 cites Proverbs 22:19–21
and Luke 1:3–4 among its proofs. **[inferred]** Deuteronomy 17:18 adds that
Scripture envisaged the law being copied, from an exemplar in the priests'
keeping, and treated the copy as "this law".

- **Isaiah 59:21** is the fullest promise that God's words will remain with
  a people across generations: "my words which I have put
  in thy mouth shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of
  thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed, from henceforth and for
  ever." The promise is of the Spirit and the words together, in the mouth.
  The Greek lacks "thy seed's seed" (see
  [hebrew-and-greek.md](hebrew-and-greek.md#differences-that-change-the-claim)).
- **Deuteronomy 31:19–21** is the other. God tells Moses to write the Song
  and teach it, "for it shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their
  seed" (כִּי לֹא תִשָּׁכַח מִפִּי זַרְעוֹ). The object is one song, and it is
  kept as a witness against Israel's unfaithfulness. The Greek strengthens
  the negative (οὐ γὰρ μὴ ἐπιλησθῇ) and adds "from their mouth".
- **The law's own institutions of transmission.** It is to be taught to
  children (Deuteronomy 6:7), read to the whole people every seventh year
  "that their children, which have not known any thing, may hear"
  (31:10–13), and passed from fathers to "the generation to come, even the
  children which should be born" (Psalm 78:5–6). After the exile Ezra reads
  it from morning to midday and the Levites "gave the sense" (Nehemiah 8:8),
  and Daniel finds Jeremiah's prophecy "in the books" (Daniel 9:2).
  Malachi, the last of the prophets, closes with "Remember ye the law of
  Moses" (4:4).
- **Psalm 12:7** is the passage most often cited for the preservation of the
  words. In the Hebrew, "thou shalt keep *them*" (תִּשְׁמְרֵם) is followed by
  "thou shalt preserve *him*" (תִּצְּרֶנּוּ), and the nearest plural antecedent
  is the poor and needy of verse 5. The Greek reads "thou shalt keep *us*,
  and preserve *us*" (φυλάξεις ἡμᾶς καὶ διατηρήσεις ἡμᾶς). **[inferred]** The
  reading "the words" is possible in the Hebrew but not required, and is
  excluded by the Greek.
- **John 14:26** promises the apostles that the Spirit will "bring to your
  remembrance all that I said to you" (ὑπομνήσει ὑμᾶς πάντα ἃ εἶπον ὑμῖν): a
  promise about the apostles' memory, the means by which Jesus's words
  reached writing.
- **Jesus treats the written word as addressed to its later readers.**
  "Have ye not read that which was spoken unto you by God?" (Matthew 22:31),
  of Exodus 3:6; "they have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them"
  (Luke 16:29). He prays for those "which shall believe on me through their
  word" (John 17:20), and sends the apostles to teach "all things
  whatsoever I have commanded you", with the promise "I am with you alway,
  even unto the end of the world" (Matthew 28:20).
- **The apostles' handing on.** Paul "delivered … that which I also
  received" (1 Corinthians 15:3) and tells Timothy to commit what he heard
  "to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2);
  Hebrews says the salvation spoken by the Lord "was confirmed unto us by
  them that heard him" (2:3); 1 Peter 1:12 says the prophets ministered
  "not unto themselves, but unto us"; Colossians 4:16 has letters exchanged
  between churches.
- **Romans 15:4**, **4:23–24**, **1 Corinthians 9:10; 10:11** and
  **Deuteronomy 29:29** say the word was written "for our learning" and
  belongs "to us and to our children for ever", which presupposes that it
  would reach them.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Scripture promises that God's word will
remain with his people. Where it says how, it names the Spirit, the mouth,
memory, teaching, public reading, writing, and handing on from one
generation of teachers to the next. It does not say that the written form
will be kept without variation.

### Keeping

[John 8:31, 51; 14:23; 17:6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-8-31);
[Mark 4:15; Luke 8:12, 15](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#mark-4-15);
[Revelation 1:3; 22:7, 9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-1-3);
[2 Thessalonians 2:2, 15; 3:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2thess-2-2);
[1 Timothy 6:20; 2 Timothy 1:13–14](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1tim-6-20);
[Titus 1:9; Colossians 3:16; 1 Corinthians 11:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#titus-1-9);
[1 Corinthians 15:1–4](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1cor-15-1);
[1 John 2:14, 24; 2 John 2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1john-2-14).

Where the Greek of the New Testament says the word is "kept", believers keep
it. Jesus: "If ye continue (μείνητε) in my word, then are ye my disciples
indeed" (John 8:31); "if a man keep (τηρήσῃ) my saying, he shall never see
death" (8:51); "they have kept thy word" (17:6). The good ground "having
heard the word, keep it" (κατέχουσιν, Luke 8:15). Paul: "keep in memory
what I preached unto you" (1 Corinthians 15:2), "hold the traditions which
ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle" (2 Thessalonians
2:15), "holding fast the faithful word" (Titus 1:9), "let the word of Christ
dwell in you richly" (Colossians 3:16). Timothy is told "keep that which is
committed to thy trust" (τὴν παραθήκην φύλαξον, 1 Timothy 6:20), and to
keep it "by the Holy Ghost which dwelleth in us" (2 Timothy 1:14).
Revelation blesses "he that keepeth the sayings of the prophecy of this
book" (1:3; 22:7).

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** This is the human side of preservation, and
the New Testament speaks of it more often than of any other means. The
keepers are the church and its teachers; the object is the teaching
received, spoken or written; and the keeping is by memory, obedience and
teaching, with the Spirit's help (2 Timothy 1:14). It is a duty laid on
believers, with the warning that the word can be taken from a hearer (Mark
4:15).

### Purity and truth

[2 Samuel 7:25, 28; 1 Chronicles 17:23](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2sam-7-25);
[2 Samuel 22:31; Psalm 18:30](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2sam-22-31);
[Psalm 12:6–7](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-12-6);
[Psalm 19:9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-19-9);
[Psalm 33:4, 6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-33-4);
[Psalm 119:140](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-119-140);
[Psalm 119:142, 144](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-119-142);
[Proverbs 22:19–21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#prov-22-19);
[Proverbs 30:5–6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#prov-30-5);
[John 17:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-17-17);
[1 Peter 2:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1pet-2-2);
[Revelation 19:9; 21:5; 22:6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-22-6).

The Old Testament image is metallurgical: God's word is "refined"
(צְרוּפָה; πεπυρωμένον, "tried in the fire"), like "silver tried in a furnace,
purified seven times" (Psalm 12:6). **[inferred]** The image is of something
without dross, tested and found true: it describes the word as God speaks
it, not the state of copies. The New Testament drops the image and speaks
of truth (ἀλήθεια, John 17:17; ἀληθινοί, Revelation 19:9; 21:5; 22:6) and of "the
unadulterated milk of the word" (τὸ λογικὸν ἄδολον γάλα, 1 Peter 2:2),
ἄδολος being the word for goods not cut with anything else (see
[greek-consistency.md §7](greek-consistency.md#7-pure-tried-πυρόω-and-ἁγνός-and-what-replaces-them)).

The Old Testament also says it plainly: "the word of the LORD is right"
(Psalm 33:4), "thy law is the truth" (119:142), "thy words be true" (2
Samuel 7:28), "the certainty of the words of truth" (Proverbs 22:21).

**Assessment.** These are claims about the word's quality: it is true, and
it contains nothing false. They are the proper scriptural ground for the
"purity" of Scripture as a quality of God's word (as in WLC Q4). They are not
claims about textual transmission.

The word is "living" (ζῶν, Hebrews 4:12; λόγια ζῶντα, Acts 7:38), "works"
in believers (ἐνεργεῖται, 1 Thessalonians 2:13), is "not bound" (οὐ δέδεται,

able to save your souls" (James 1:18, 21); "faith cometh by hearing, and
hearing by the word of God" (Romans 10:17). **Assessment.** These passages make the word's endurance
active: it is not only kept, it does what God sends it to do. That is the
reading of preservation the Lutheran confessions favour (see
[confessions.md](confessions.md#book-of-concord)).

### Prohibition of alteration

[Deuteronomy 4:2; 12:32](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-4-2);
[Proverbs 30:5–6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#prov-30-5);
[Jeremiah 26:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-26-2);
[Revelation 22:18–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-22-18);
[Galatians 1:6–9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#gal-1-8);
[Galatians 3:15, 17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#gal-3-15).

"Ye shall not add unto the word … neither shall ye diminish ought from it"
(Deuteronomy 4:2; οὐ προσθήσετε … οὐκ ἀφελεῖτε). Revelation 22:18–19 attaches
the plagues of the book to adding and the loss of one's part to taking away.
Paul applies the same principle to the gospel: some "would pervert
(μεταστρέψαι) the gospel of Christ. But though we, or an angel from heaven,
preach any other gospel … let him be accursed" (Galatians 1:7–8); and
argues that even a man's ratified covenant no one "addeth thereto"
(ἐπιδιατάσσεται, 3:15).

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** These are commands to people, with sanctions.
A prohibition presupposes that the forbidden act is possible; so these
passages, more than any others, show that Scripture did *not* regard the
alteration of God's words by men as impossible. Deuteronomy's command
concerns the commandments Moses was giving (what Israel was to do);
Revelation's concerns "the words of the prophecy of this book"; Galatians's,
the gospel preached. None is a promise that alteration will not happen.

### Custody

[Deuteronomy 17:18–19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-17-18);
[Deuteronomy 31:9–13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-31-9);
[Deuteronomy 31:24–26](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-31-24);
[1 Samuel 10:25](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1sam-10-25);
[2 Kings 22:8, 11, 13; 23:2–3; 2 Chronicles 34:14–15, 21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2kgs-22-8);
[Psalm 147:15, 19–20](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-147-15);
[Isaiah 8:16, 20](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-8-16);
[Jeremiah 32:10–14](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-32-14);
[Daniel 12:4, 9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#dan-12-4);
[Malachi 2:7–8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#mal-2-7);
[Revelation 22:10](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rev-22-10);
[Romans 3:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#rom-3-2);
[2 Thessalonians 2:2, 15; 3:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2thess-2-2);
[1 Timothy 6:20; 2 Timothy 1:13–14](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1tim-6-20);
[2 Timothy 4:13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2tim-4-13).

The book of the law is laid beside the ark "for a witness" (Deuteronomy
31:26); the testimony is sealed "among my disciples" (Isaiah 8:16, Hebrew
only); Daniel's book is sealed "to the time of the end"; and to the Jews "were
committed the oracles of God" (ἐπιστεύθησαν τὰ λόγια τοῦ θεοῦ, Romans 3:2),
as "he sheweth his word unto Jacob … he hath not dealt so with any nation"
(Psalm 147:19–20). Moses gives the written law to "the priests the sons of
Levi … and unto all the elders of Israel" (Deuteronomy 31:9), and the king's
copy is made from the one "before the priests" (17:18); "the priest's lips
should keep knowledge" (Malachi 2:7). Samuel lays his book "up before the
LORD" (1 Samuel 10:25), and God has Jeremiah store deeds in an earthen jar
"that they may continue many days" (Jeremiah 32:14). In the New Testament
the deposit (παραθήκη) is entrusted to Timothy (1 Timothy 6:20), Paul asks
for "the books, but especially the parchments" (2 Timothy 4:13), and gives
his own handwriting as the mark of a genuine letter (2 Thessalonians 3:17).
Revelation is not to be sealed (22:10), the reverse of Daniel.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Scripture assigns the written word to human
keepers: priests, elders, kings, prophets, disciples, a nation, and in the
New Testament the church's teachers. Its keepers could fail: the priests of
Malachi's day "corrupted the covenant of Levi" (2:8), and the book of the law
went unread in the temple until Josiah (see [Loss](#loss)). Romans 3:2 says the oracles were
*entrusted* (πιστεύω), a word that implies a duty of faithful keeping, not a
guarantee of it; the next verse asks "what if some were unfaithful?"
(ἠπίστησαν). Romans 3:2 is the text the Helvetic Consensus (Canon II) and
the Second London Confession (1.8) cite for the Hebrew text.

### Restoration

[Exodus 34:1; Deuteronomy 10:2, 4](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#exod-34-1);
[2 Kings 22:8, 11, 13; 23:2–3; 2 Chronicles 34:14–15, 21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2kgs-22-8);
[Ezra 7:6, 10; Nehemiah 8:1–3, 8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#neh-8-8);
[Jeremiah 36:2, 27–28, 32](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-36).

The first tables are broken and God writes the same words again "according
to the first writing" (Deuteronomy 10:4). Jehoiakim burns Jeremiah's scroll
column by column, and God commands that "all the former words" be written
again; "and there were added besides unto them many like words" (Jeremiah
36:32). Under Josiah a book of the law, neglected and unknown, is found in
the temple and read to the people (2 Kings 22–23); after the exile Ezra reads
the law to the returned community (Nehemiah 8).

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** The first two are the only narratives in
Scripture of a written word of God being destroyed, and in both God's
answer is to have it written again. In the second, the restored text is *longer* than the
lost one. Scripture's own model of preservation here is not an unbroken
chain of copies but renewed writing after loss. In the other two, a book
that had been neglected is recovered and read again.

### Loss

[2 Kings 22:8, 11, 13; 23:2–3; 2 Chronicles 34:14–15, 21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2kgs-22-8);
[Numbers 21:14; Joshua 10:13; 2 Samuel 1:18; 1 Kings 11:41; 1 Chronicles 29:29; 2 Chronicles 9:29; 12:15; 13:22](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2chr-9-29);
[1 Corinthians 5:9; Colossians 4:16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1cor-5-9).

- **The book found in the temple** (2 Kings 22:8–13; 2 Chronicles
  34:14–21). Hilkiah finds "the book of the law in the house of the LORD";
  when it is read Josiah tears his clothes "because our fathers have not
  hearkened unto the words of this book". **[inferred]** The narrative
  presupposes that in the temple itself, under the kings, the book had gone
  unread long enough for its finding to be news. Scripture does not say how
  long, nor whether other copies existed.
- **Books cited and not preserved.** The historical books cite "the book of
  the wars of the LORD" (Numbers 21:14), "the book of Jasher" (Joshua 10:13;
  2 Samuel 1:18), "the book of the acts of Solomon" (1 Kings 11:41), and
  writings of the prophets Samuel, Nathan, Gad, Ahijah, Iddo and Shemaiah (1
  Chronicles 29:29; 2 Chronicles 9:29; 12:15; 13:22). **[context]** None is
  extant or in any canon.
- **An apostolic letter not preserved.** "I wrote unto you in an epistle"
  (1 Corinthians 5:9); and "the epistle from Laodicea" (Colossians 4:16).
  **[context]** Neither is extant under that name.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** Scripture records that writings, including
writings of prophets and an apostle, were lost, and that a book of the law
was for a time lost to use in the temple. It never calls the lost prophetic
or apostolic writings Scripture, and it never promises that every writing
of a prophet or apostle would survive. What it promises to keep is God's
word; what it records as lost are particular writings. A doctrine of
preservation consistent with Scripture has to allow that God did not keep
everything his servants wrote.

### Corruption and withdrawal

[Psalm 119:43, 111](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-119-43);
[Isaiah 29:10–14](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-29-11);
[Jeremiah 8:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-8-8);
[Jeremiah 23:28–29, 36](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-23-28);
[Hosea 8:12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#hos-8-12);
[Amos 8:11–12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#amos-8-11);
[Malachi 2:7–8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#mal-2-7);
[Mark 4:15; Luke 8:12, 15](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#mark-4-15);
[Matthew 15:6; Mark 7:13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-15-6);
[2 Thessalonians 2:2, 15; 3:17](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2thess-2-2);
[2 Corinthians 2:17; 4:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2cor-2-17);
[2 Corinthians 3:14–15](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2cor-3-14);
[Galatians 1:6–9](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#gal-1-8);
[2 Peter 3:16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2pet-3-16).

- **Jeremiah 8:8**: "How do ye say, We are wise, and the law of the LORD is
  with us? Lo, certainly in vain made he it; the pen of the scribes is in
  vain" (KJV). The Hebrew is sharper: "the lying pen of the scribes has made
  it into a lie" (לַשֶּׁקֶר עָשָׂה עֵט שֶׁקֶר סֹפְרִים). **[inferred]** Whether
  this charges the scribes with miscopying the law or with false teaching
  in writing is disputed; either way, God says that what scribes wrote
  under the name of his law was false.
- **Jeremiah 23:36**: "ye have perverted (הֲפַכְתֶּם) the words of the living
  God" (Hebrew only).
- **Matthew 15:6**: "ye have made the word of God of none effect
  (ἠκυρώσατε) by your tradition". The text stands; its force is cancelled.
- **2 Corinthians 2:17**: "we are not as many, which corrupt (καπηλεύοντες,
  peddle, adulterate as a huckster waters wine) the word of God"; 4:2,
  "nor handling the word of God deceitfully" (δολοῦντες).
- **2 Peter 3:16**: the unlearned "wrest" (στρεβλοῦσιν, twist) Paul's letters
  "as they do also the other scriptures".
- **Isaiah 29:10–13**: the vision becomes "as the words of a book that is
  sealed", which neither learned nor unlearned can read, and the people's
  fear of God "is taught by the precept of men". Jesus applies 29:13 to the
  tradition that annulled the commandment (Matthew 15:8–9).
- **Hosea 8:12**: "I have written to him the great things of my law, but
  they were counted as a strange thing."
- **Malachi 2:8**: the priests, the law's appointed keepers, "have caused
  many to stumble at the law; ye have corrupted (שִׁחַתֶּם, διεφθείρατε)
  the covenant of Levi".
- **Galatians 1:7** and **2 Thessalonians 2:2**: some "would pervert the
  gospel"; a letter circulated "as from us", which Paul answers with the
  sign of his own hand (3:17). This is the one New Testament warning against
  a forged writing.
- **Amos 8:11**: God will send "a famine of hearing the words of the LORD;
  they shall seek the word of the LORD, and shall not find it."
- **Withdrawal from a hearer or reader**: "take not the word of truth
  utterly out of my mouth" (Psalm 119:43); Satan "taketh away the word that
  was sown in their hearts" (Mark 4:15); "until this day remaineth the same
  vail untaken away in the reading of the old testament" (2 Corinthians
  3:14).

**Assessment.** Scripture does not claim that God's word is safe from human
corruption; it repeatedly says the opposite. The corruptions it names are of
teaching, interpretation and practice more than of copies (Jeremiah 8:8 may
be about writing, and 2 Thessalonians 2:2 is about a forged letter), and its
remedy is the prophet's and the
apostle's faithful word, not a guarantee about manuscripts. Amos adds that
God may judge a people by withdrawing his word from them; Isaiah 29 and 2
Corinthians 3 add that the text may remain present and read while its sense
is closed to its readers. **[inferred]** A
doctrine of preservation consistent with Scripture has to allow for both.

### Origin

[Ecclesiastes 12:9–12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#eccl-12-9);
[Ezekiel 2:9–3:3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ezek-2-9);
[Matthew 22:31–32; Mark 12:26](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-22-31);
[John 5:39, 45–47](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#john-5-39);
[1 Peter 1:10–12](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1pet-1-10);
[2 Timothy 3:15–16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2tim-3-16);
[2 Peter 1:19–21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#2pet-1-19);
[Hebrews 1:1–2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#heb-1-1);
[1 Corinthians 14:37](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#1cor-14-37);
[Acts 1:16; 3:18, 21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#acts-1-16).

"All scripture is God-breathed" (πᾶσα γραφὴ θεόπνευστος); prophecy came not
by the will of man but as men "were borne along by the Holy Spirit"
(φερόμενοι). Hebrews opens with God having "spoken … by the prophets" and
"by his Son" (1:1–2); Peter says the Holy Spirit spoke "by the mouth of
David" (Acts 1:16) and that "the Spirit of Christ" was in the prophets (1
Peter 1:11); Paul, that "the things that I write unto you are the
commandments of the Lord" (1 Corinthians 14:37). Jesus calls words written
in Exodus "that which was spoken unto you by God" (Matthew 22:31), and
Ecclesiastes says the words of the wise "are given from one shepherd"
(12:11). **Assessment.** These are claims about where Scripture comes
from, not how it is kept. They are included because the confessions join
inspiration and preservation in one sentence ("immediately inspired by God,
and by his singular care and providence kept pure", WCF 1.8). **[inferred]**
2 Timothy 3:15–16 does show that Paul regarded the Scriptures Timothy had
known "from a child", copies in use in the first century, and very likely
the Greek ones, as "God-breathed" and able to make him wise to salvation.

### Exaltation

[Psalm 138:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-138-2). "Thou hast
magnified thy word above all thy name" (Hebrew); "thou hast magnified thy
holy name above every thing" (Greek). The exaltation of the word is in the
Hebrew only.

## What the passages do not say

**[inferred]** Taking the 151 passages together:

1. **No passage names a text or manuscript tradition** as the form in
   which God's word will endure, and only one bears on its language:
   Matthew 5:18 speaks of the Law's iota, a Greek letter. Taking the Greek
   as the text, that counts for the Greek. The question "preserved in the
   Hebrew or in the Greek?" is weighed in the companion study
   [preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md),
   which rates Matthew 5:18 strong for the Greek and weak for the Hebrew.
2. **No passage promises that copies of Scripture will be free of error.**
   The nearest candidates are Matthew 5:18, Luke 16:17 and John 10:35, which
   are about authority; and Psalm 12:7, whose object is disputed in the
   Hebrew and is "us" in the Greek. The one command to copy the law
   (Deuteronomy 17:18) says what the copy is made from, not that it will be
   exact.
3. **No Greek verb in either Testament has God "keeping" a text.** God keeps
   people (φυλάσσω, τηρέω), and keeps his covenant and promise in the sense
   of performing it (1 Kings 8:24, ἃ ἐφύλαξας); people keep the word (see
   [greek-consistency.md §8](greek-consistency.md#8-keep-preserve-φυλάσσω-and-τηρέω)).
4. **The promises of transmission are to the mouths and hearts of a
   people.** Isaiah 59:21 and Deuteronomy 31:21 speak of the mouth, not of a
   book; Deuteronomy 31:21's object is one song.
5. **Many passages show the means by which God kept his word**: writing,
   copying, deposit, public reading, teaching children, a people entrusted
   with it, the Spirit's work in memory and in the mouth, handing on by
   teachers, rewriting after loss, and the keeping of the word by
   believers.
6. **Several passages admit corruption and loss among men**, and the
   commands not to add or take away presuppose it is possible. Scripture
   records prophetic and apostolic writings that were not preserved, a book
   of the law neglected in the temple, and a forged apostolic letter.

## Assessment

**[inferred]** Three readings of a doctrine of preservation are possible
(the companion study sets them out in
[§8](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#8-what-this-means-for-the-preservation-claims)):

| Reading | What it holds | How the passages bear on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Message and efficacy** | God's word, its content and power, abides and accomplishes its purpose among his people | **Stated directly** and often: fulfilment, permanence, efficacy, transmission, keeping, Isaiah 59:21, Deuteronomy 31:21, 1 Peter 1:25 |
| **Providential sufficiency** | God by his providence keeps the Scriptures in a state sufficient for faith and life, through means and despite errors in copies | **Consonant**: inscripturation, copying, custody, restoration, the apostolic handing on, and the inviolability sayings fit it; no passage states it in those words, and none contradicts it |
| **Verbal preservation** | God keeps a particular text, to its letters, free from error in every age | **Not stated by any passage.** It can be inferred only by reading Matthew 5:18 or Psalm 12:7 as promises about copies, which their Greek (and, for Psalm 12, the Greek translation) does not support; and it sits uneasily with the passages that admit corruption and forgery and record loss and rewriting |

Scripture's own claim is therefore strong in one direction and silent in
another. It claims, more often and more emphatically than almost anything
else it says about itself, that God's word will not fail, pass away or be
annulled. It does not claim to know which copies will carry it, and it
expects the people who carry it to be capable of falsifying it. How the
confessions handle that gap is the subject of [confessions.md](confessions.md).
