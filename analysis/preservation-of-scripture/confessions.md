# The Confessions' Claims Measured Against Scripture's

Part of [The Preservation of Scripture in Scripture and in the Confessions](README.md).

This page takes every confessional document in the repository (listed in
[catalog.yaml](../../catalog.yaml)), sets out what it says about the
endurance, purity or keeping of God's word, and asks whether that is
**consonant** with what Scripture says about itself, **falls short** of it,
**exceeds** it, or **misinterprets** it. The scriptural side is the
collection in [passages.md](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md) and its
analysis in [README.md](README.md). Every place where a confession cites one
of those passages is listed in
[confessional-citations.md](confessional-citations.md), which is generated
and will pick up a new confession automatically.

The companion study
[preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md)
(§9) assesses the confessions' claims about the *Hebrew and Greek text* in
the light of that text's history. This page assesses all of their claims
about the word's endurance against *Scripture's own claims*. The two agree
where they overlap.

**[context]** marks a statement resting on history outside the texts;
**[inferred]** marks this study's own reading.

## The verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Consonant** | Says what Scripture says, or draws an inference Scripture plainly supports |
| **Falls short** | Says less than Scripture says on a point the document addresses |
| **Exceeds** | Says more than any passage of Scripture says |
| **Misinterprets** | Rests the claim on a passage that, read in its own language and context, does not say it |
| **Silent** | Makes no claim about the endurance or keeping of the word |

A statement can earn two verdicts: a claim can exceed Scripture *and* rest
on a misread proof text.

## Westminster Confession 1.8: "kept pure in all ages"

> The Old Testament in Hebrew (which was the native language of the people
> of God of old), and the New Testament in Greek (which at the time of the
> writing of it was most generally known to the nations), being immediately
> inspired by God, and by his singular care and providence kept pure in all
> ages, are therefore authentical;[a] so as in all controversies of religion
> the Church is finally to appeal unto them.[b] But because these original
> tongues are not known to all the people of God … therefore they are to be
> translated into the vulgar language of every nation unto which they
> come…
>
> [a] Matt 5:18 · [b] Isa 8:20; John 5:39, 46; Acts 15:15
>
> — [Westminster Confession of Faith 1.8](../../confessions/reformed/presbyterian/westminster-standards/westminster-confession-of-faith.md#1-of-the-holy-scripture)

This is the strongest statement about preservation in any confession in the
repository, and the only one that names the Hebrew and the Greek. The Savoy
Declaration repeats it word for word with the same proofs.

**What it claims.** Three things: (1) the Hebrew Old Testament and the Greek
New Testament were immediately inspired; (2) God's "singular care and
providence" has kept them "pure in all ages"; (3) *therefore* they are
"authentical", the final court of appeal.

**Its proof.** The only proof given for (1) and (2) is Matthew 5:18.

**Matthew 5:18 in the Greek.** "Until heaven and earth pass away, one iota
or one horn shall by no means pass away from the law, until all things take
place" (ἰῶτα ἓν ἢ μία κεραία οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἀπὸ τοῦ νόμου, ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται).
Read in its own language:

- the verb (παρέρχομαι) is the one used of heaven and earth in the same
  sentence: to cease to stand, not to be miscopied;
- "iota" and "horn" mean the least letter and the least stroke; ἰῶτα is a
  Greek letter, so the Law the verse speaks of is measured in Greek
  letters, not Hebrew ones;
- verse 19 draws the consequence with "therefore": whoever "loosens" (λύσῃ)
  one of the least commandments. What does not pass away is the binding
  force of the least commandment;
- the object is "the law" (with the prophets, v. 17), not the New
  Testament, which did not yet exist;
- the claim is bounded: until heaven and earth pass and "all things take
  place".

(The full reading is in
[preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md §1.1](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#11-matthew-518-read-in-the-greek)
and in the [passage notes](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#matt-5-18).)

**Assessment.**

1. **The proof is misinterpreted.** **[inferred]** Matthew 5:18 says the
   law cannot be annulled; WCF 1.8 uses it to say copies of the Hebrew and
   Greek have been kept pure. The verse supports the *authority* half of 1.8
   ("authentical", the Church's final appeal) much better than the
   *preservation* half it is attached to. It says nothing about the New
   Testament or about scribes. And so far as it speaks of letters at all,
   the letter it names is the Greek iota: read as a proof about letters, it
   tells for the Greek, while 1.8 uses it chiefly to establish the Hebrew
   Old Testament as "authentical".
2. **The claim exceeds Scripture if read of the letters.** No passage
   promises that the text of Scripture will be kept free of error in all
   ages (see [README.md, "What the passages do not say"](README.md#what-the-passages-do-not-say)).
   Scripture itself records the loss of the written law (2 Kings 22),
   the destruction and rewriting of a prophet's book with additions
   (Jeremiah 36), "the lying pen of the scribes" (Jeremiah 8:8), the
   perversion of God's words (Jeremiah 23:36), and a famine of the word
   (Amos 8:11). "In all ages" is stronger than anything the passages say.
3. **The claim is consonant if read of the substance.** If "kept pure"
   means that God has kept his word, its message and its authority,
   available to his people in the Hebrew and Greek Scriptures, with no
   promise about each copy's letters, it agrees with Scripture's permanence
   and transmission passages, and above all with Isaiah 59:21. The
   companion study reaches the same conclusion from the side of the history
   of the text.
   **[context]** Which of these the divines meant is debated. John Owen,
   writing thirteen years later in defence of the same doctrine, held "the
   whole word of God, in every tittle of it, preserved entire in the copies
   of the original extant" while granting "various lections in the Old
   Testament and the New" (see
   [canon-in-the-theologians.md](../canon-in-the-theologians.md#owen)).
4. **The better proofs were available, and Westminster cited them
   elsewhere.** The same Confession cites Isaiah 59:21 at 25.3 for Christ's
   giving "the ministry, oracles, and ordinances of God" to the Church "to
   the end of the world", which is the closest scriptural basis for
   preservation that exists, and uses it correctly there. Isaiah 40:8,
   1 Peter 1:23–25, Matthew 24:35 and Psalm 119:89 are not cited anywhere in
   the Westminster Standards.
5. **The rest of 1.8 is consonant, with one irony.** The appeal to
   Scripture as final (Isa 8:20; John 5:39, 46) is well grounded; but
   Isaiah 8:20's "to the law and to the testimony" is in the Hebrew only
   (the Greek reads differently), and Acts 15:15 introduces a quotation of
   Amos 9:11–12 in a form close to the Septuagint, which differs from the
   Hebrew (see the companion study
   [§3.3](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#33-where-the-consonants-allow-more-than-one-reading)). The command to translate agrees
   with 1 Peter 1:25 ("the word which by the gospel is preached unto you"):
   Scripture treats the word preached in another language as the word of
   God.

**Verdict.** "Kept pure in all ages" **exceeds** what Scripture says if it
is a claim about the letters of a text, and its sole proof is
**misinterpreted**. Read as a claim about the substance of God's word, it is
**consonant**, but it would then be better proved from Isaiah 59:21 and
Isaiah 40:8 than from Matthew 5:18.

## The Westminster Standards

**Westminster Confession 1.1.** God was pleased "for the better preserving
and propagating of the truth … to commit the same wholly unto writing."
Proofs: Prov 22:19–21; Isa 8:19–20; Matt 4:4, 7, 10; Luke 1:3–4; Rom 15:4.
**Consonant.** It names writing as the means of preservation, exactly as
the inscripturation passages do (Exodus 17:14; Isaiah 30:8; Jeremiah 30:2;
Habakkuk 2:2), though it cites none of those. Three of its proofs are in the
collection and fit: Proverbs 22:19–21 ("Have not I written to thee …, that I
might make thee know the certainty of the words of truth?"), Luke 1:1–4
(written "that thou mightest know the certainty", ἀσφάλειαν), and Romans
15:4 ("written for our learning"). **[inferred]** Proverbs 22:20 fits in the
Hebrew, where the sage has written; in the Greek the reader is told to copy
the words out, which would suit the "propagating" half of 1.1's sentence.

**Westminster Confession 1.6.** "Unto which nothing at any time is to be
added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men."
Proofs: Gal 1:8–9; 2 Thess 2:2; 2 Tim 3:15–17. **Consonant.** The
prohibitions of Deuteronomy 4:2 and Revelation 22:18–19 are not cited here
(they are cited at 1.2, 21.1 and WLC Q3), but the principle is theirs, and
Galatians 1:8–9 is its New Testament form. 2 Thessalonians 2:2 is the one
passage in Scripture that warns against a forged apostolic letter; 1.6 uses
it against a spurious "word" claiming the apostles' authority, which is what
it says.

**Westminster Confession 1.8, its other proofs.** Besides Matthew 5:18,
1.8 cites John 5:39 ("search the scriptures") for the appeal to the
originals in controversies, and Colossians 3:16 ("let the word of Christ
dwell in you richly") for translation into the vulgar tongue. **Consonant**
in both uses: they are proofs of what the faithful are to do with the word,
which is how the Greek speaks of keeping it (see
[README.md, Keeping](README.md#keeping)).

**Westminster Confession 25.3.** Christ has given "the ministry, oracles,
and ordinances of God" to the Church "to the end of the world: and doth by
his own presence and Spirit, according to his promise, make them
effectual." Proofs: Isa 59:21; Matt 28:19–20; 1 Cor 12:28; Eph 4:11–13.
**Consonant.** This is the Standards' best-grounded statement of
preservation: the oracles remain with the Church by Christ's promise, and
the promises cited are the ones Scripture actually makes, Isaiah 59:21 and
"I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world" (Matthew 28:20), whose
phrase "to the end of the world" the article takes over. 1.5 also cites Isaiah
59:21, for the Spirit's witness with the word; that too fits the verse,
which joins "my spirit" and "my words".

**Larger Catechism.** Q3 cites Rev 22:18–19 for Scripture as the "only rule",
and Luke 16:29 ("they have Moses and the prophets"). Q4 says the Scriptures
show themselves to be God's word "by their majesty and purity", citing
Hosea 8:12 ("I have written to him the great things of my law"), Psalm 12:6
and 119:140. **Consonant**: these are the passages about
the word's refined, true quality, and the catechism uses them for that, not
for transmission. (Psalm 12:7, the contested verse, is not cited by any
confession in the repository.) Q113 counts among the sins of the third
commandment "perverting" the word, citing Jeremiah 23:36 and 2 Peter 3:16,
and Q159 requires preachers to speak the word "faithfully" (Jer 23:28) and
not corrupt it (2 Cor 2:17). **Consonant**: the catechism takes over
Scripture's own admission that men pervert the word. Q156 cites Isaiah 34:16
("seek ye out of the book of the LORD") for the reading of Scripture by all,
a Hebrew-only reading. **Q156–Q158** gather the law's own institutions of
transmission and use them as Scripture uses them: the king's reading of his
copy (Deut 17:19), the law delivered to the priests and read to all (Deut
31:9), the fathers teaching their children (Ps 78:5–7; Deut 6:6–9), Ezra's
public reading (Neh 8:2–3), Josiah's response to the found book (2 Chr
34:21), the priest's lips keeping knowledge (Mal 2:7), and the veil on the
reading of Moses (2 Cor 3:13–16). **Consonant**; **[inferred]** it is the
fullest use of the transmission passages in any confession, though it uses
them for the duty of reading and preaching, not for preservation. Q113
cites 1 Timothy 6:20 for "vain janglings", not for the deposit it speaks of.

**Shorter Catechism.** "The Word of God, which is contained in the
Scriptures" (Q2). **Silent** on preservation.

**Form of Government, Directories.** No claim about preservation. The
Directory for the Publick Worship of God orders Scripture to be read "in the
vulgar tongue, out of the best allowed translation", presupposing, like
WCF 1.8's last sentence, that the word read in translation is God's word.
**Silent**; the presupposition is consonant with 1 Peter 1:25.

## Savoy, the Second London Confession and Keach's Catechism

**Savoy Declaration (1658).** Chapter 1 is Westminster's verbatim, with the
same proofs, including Matthew 5:18 at 1.8 and Isaiah 59:21 at 1.5. Its
chapters on the church are its own, and do not repeat WCF 25.3.
**Verdicts as Westminster** for chapter 1.

**Second London Confession (1677/1689).** 1.1 strengthens Westminster's
opening ("the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule") and keeps the
sentence on committing the truth to writing: **consonant**. 1.8 keeps
"kept pure in all ages", but its proof list (Rom 3:2; Isa 8:20; Acts 15:15;
John 5:39; 1 Cor 14; Col 3:16) drops Matthew 5:18 and begins with Romans
3:2, "unto them were committed the oracles of God".

**[inferred]** That is a better-chosen proof: Romans 3:2 is about the
custody of the Old Testament, and the verb (ἐπιστεύθησαν, "were entrusted")
fits a claim about the Hebrew text. But it is a statement of trust given,
not of trust kept; Paul's next words are "what if some did not believe
(ἠπίστησαν)?", and it says nothing of the New Testament or the Greek.
**Verdict:** 1.8 still **exceeds** Scripture if read of the letters, but
its proof is not misapplied.

**Keach's Catechism (1693).** The Scriptures are "the only infallible rule
of faith and practice" (Q4), known to be God's word by their doctrine,
unity and power (Q5, citing Ps 19:7–9; Heb 4:12). **Consonant.**

## The Three Forms of Unity

**Belgic Confession (1561).**

- **Art. 3**: God "commanded his servants, the prophets and apostles, to
  commit his revealed word to writing; and he himself wrote with his own
  finger, the two tables of the law." **Consonant**: inscripturation
  (Exodus 17:14; 34:1; Deuteronomy 10:4).
- **Art. 5**: "the very blind are able to perceive that the things foretold
  in them are fulfilling." **Consonant**: the fulfilment passages, the
  largest category in Scripture.
- **Art. 7**: "since it is forbidden, to add unto or take away anything from
  the word of God, it doth thereby evidently appear that the doctrine
  thereof is most perfect and complete in all respects." **Consonant**, with
  a note: **[inferred]** the prohibition is used to prove *sufficiency*, not
  preservation. The inference (if nothing may be added, nothing is lacking)
  is reasonable, and the Belgic does not go further and claim the text
  cannot be altered, which the prohibition would contradict.

The Belgic makes no claim about the preservation of the text or the
languages. It cites no Scripture proofs in the edition in this repository,
which is why [confessional-citations.md](confessional-citations.md) lists
none for it.

**Heidelberg Catechism (1563).** Q54: "the Son of God from the beginning to
the end of the world, gathers, defends, and preserves to himself by his
Spirit and word … a church", citing Isaiah 59:21. **Consonant**, and the
most exact use of Isaiah 59:21 in any confession: the verse promises that the
Spirit and the words will not depart from the mouth of the prophet's seed,
and the catechism reads it as the Son keeping his people by Spirit and word.
Q19 (the gospel "revealed in Paradise", published by patriarchs and
prophets, and "fulfilled" by the Son, citing Romans 1:2) states transmission
and fulfilment. **Consonant.** Q103 cites 2 Timothy 2:2 for the maintenance
of the ministry of the gospel. **Consonant**: the verse is about passing the
teaching to "faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also".

**Canons of Dort (1618–1619).** V.14: God "preserves, continues, and
perfects" his work in the saints "by the hearing and reading of his Word".
**Consonant**: **[inferred]** it states the pattern the Greek of both
Testaments shows: God keeps people, and keeps them by the word (see
[greek-consistency.md §8](greek-consistency.md#8-keep-preserve-φυλάσσω-and-τηρέω)).

## The Scots Confession and the Thirty-Nine Articles

**Scots Confession (1560).** Chapter 19 affirms the Scriptures sufficient
and their authority "to be of God and neither to depend on men nor angels";
chapter 20 says councils "have manifestly erred" and are to be received
only so far as they prove their decisions "by the plain Word of God".
**Silent** on preservation; what it says about authority is consonant.

**Thirty-Nine Articles (1571).** Article 20: "although the Church be a
witness and a keeper of holy Writ, yet, as it ought not to decree any thing
against the same, so besides the same ought it not to enforce any thing to
be believed for necessity of Salvation." **Consonant.** **[inferred]** The
Article assigns the keeping of Scripture to the Church, as Scripture assigns
it to the Levites (Deuteronomy 31:26) and to Israel (Romans 3:2), and it
pairs custody with a prohibition on adding, as Deuteronomy 4:2 does. It
claims no more than that.

## The ecumenical creeds

The Nicene Creed confesses the Holy Spirit "who spake by the Prophets"
(origin). The Athanasian Creed says nothing on the subject. **Silent.**

## Book of Concord

The Book of Concord makes no claim about the Hebrew or Greek text, the
languages, or the copies of Scripture. What it says about the endurance of
God's word is said in passing, in arguments about other doctrines, and
always in terms of the word's truth, power and fulfilment.

The Small Catechism, the Treatise and the Book of Concord's creeds make no
claim on the subject.

**Assessment.** **[inferred]** The Book of Concord's statements are all
**consonant** with Scripture and stay close to the categories Scripture
itself uses most: fulfilment, efficacy, inviolability and permanence. The
Large Catechism's Preface (¶14) cites Deuteronomy 6:6–7 for the daily
teaching of the word in the household, the law's own means of
transmission. It
**falls short** of Scripture in one respect only: it never states the
inscripturation theme (that God commanded his word to be written so that it
would reach later generations), which Scripture states repeatedly and the
Reformed confessions state at the head of their articles on Scripture. It
also never takes up a promise of transmission such as Isaiah 59:21 or
Deuteronomy 31:21. **[context]**
The Lutheran theologians of the next century did make claims about the
text, including the inspiration of the vowel points (see
[canon-in-the-theologians.md](../canon-in-the-theologians.md)), but the Book
of Concord binds no one to them.

## Confessions not in the repository

These are listed in [catalog.yaml](../../catalog.yaml) without a text. They are
assessed here only where the claim is well known; when a text is added they
should be assessed in full.

- **Helvetic Consensus Formula (1675).** **[context]** Canon I says God has
  watched over his word since it was written "so that it could not be
  corrupted by craft of Satan or fraud of man"; Canon II, that the received
  Hebrew is inspired "not only in its consonants, but in its vowels"; Canon
  III, that the Septuagint and other versions may not correct it (quoted
  from [canon-in-the-confessions.md](../canon-in-the-confessions.md#helvetic-consensus-formula-consensus-helvetica-1675)).
  **Exceeds.** **[inferred]** Canon I's "could not be corrupted" goes beyond
  every preservation passage and is in tension with those that say the
  word *was* corrupted by men (Jeremiah 8:8; 23:36). Canon II's vowel claim
  is assessed against the history of the text in
  [preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md §9](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#9-the-confessions-text-claims-in-this-light);
  against Scripture it has no passage at all, since no passage speaks of
  vowels. Canon III conflicts with the New Testament's own quotation of the
  Septuagint where it differs from the Hebrew (Isaiah 40:6–8 in 1 Peter
  1:24–25; Deuteronomy 8:3 in Matthew 4:4).
- **Irish Articles (1615).** Scripture to be read "in the original tongues"
  and translated (Art. 4; per the companion study). No preservation claim
  is known; not assessed.
- **French (Gallican) Confession (1559), First Helvetic (1536), Second
  Helvetic (1566).** Not in the repository; not assessed.

## How the confessions use the passages

From [confessional-citations.md](confessional-citations.md):

1. **The central permanence passages are almost never cited.** Isaiah 40:8
   and 1 Peter 1:25 are cited by no confession; the Apology makes their point
   while citing the verses before (Isa 40:6; 1 Pet 1:24). Matthew 24:35 and
   its parallels are cited once (Solid Declaration VII.43, for Christ's power
   to do what he says). Psalm 119:89 is cited by none.
2. **The inviolability sayings are cited only for the law.** John 10:35 and
   Luke 16:17 are cited by no confession. Matthew 5:17–19 is cited fourteen
   times; all but two (WCF and Savoy 1.8) are for the authority of the law or
   Christ's fulfilling of it, which is what the Greek says the passage is
   about.
3. **The one transmission promise is cited, but not for the text.** Isaiah
   59:21 is cited six times: for the Spirit's witness with the word (WCF
   and Savoy 1.5), the word and Spirit as evidence that there is a God (WLC
   Q2), the preservation of the Church (Heidelberg Q54; WCF 25.3), and the
   visible church (WLC Q62). Each use fits the verse; none is at WCF 1.8.
4. **Psalm 12:7 is never cited.** Psalm 12:6 is cited once, for the
   "purity" of Scripture as a quality (WLC Q4), which is what it says.
5. **The admissions of corruption are cited as warnings.** The Larger
   Catechism cites Jeremiah 23:36, 2 Corinthians 2:17 and 2 Peter 3:16 for
   the sins of perverting and corrupting the word. Jeremiah 8:8 and Amos
   8:11 are cited by none, though the Large Catechism makes Amos's point.
   The one warning against a forged letter, 2 Thessalonians 2:2, is cited
   at Westminster and Savoy 1.6, against claims to new revelation.
6. **The other transmission promise is never cited.** Deuteronomy 31:21,
   "it shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed", appears in
   no confession, though it is God's own promise and the nearest Old
   Testament parallel to Isaiah 59:21.
7. **The means of transmission are cited for duties, not for
   preservation.** The Larger Catechism (Q156–158) and the Form of
   Government cite the king's copy, the reading of the law, Ezra's reading,
   the teaching of children and the priests' charge for the duty to read
   and preach the word. No confession cites them for the claim that God
   preserves it, though that is what they show.
8. **The records of loss are not cited for loss.** The finding of the book
   under Josiah is cited for the magistrate's duty (WCF 23.3) and for
   reverent reading (WLC Q157), not as a record that the book of the law
   had been neglected. The lost prophetic writings of Chronicles and Paul's
   earlier letter to Corinth are cited by none.
9. **Of 151 passages, 64 are cited by no confession**, including most of the
   inscripturation and fulfilment passages; they are listed at the end of
   [confessional-citations.md](confessional-citations.md#passages-no-scanned-document-cites).

## Conclusions

**[inferred]**

1. **Most of what the confessions say about the word's endurance is
   consonant with Scripture.** Writing as the means of preservation
   (Westminster 1.1, Belgic 3), the Church as keeper (Thirty-Nine Articles
   20), the Son preserving his church by Spirit and word (Heidelberg 54;
   Westminster 25.3), and the Lutheran statements that the word cannot err,
   cannot be altered by men, and will be performed all say what Scripture
   says.
2. **One statement exceeds Scripture, and rests on a misread proof**:
   "kept pure in all ages" (Westminster and Savoy 1.8), if it is read as a
   claim about the letters of the Hebrew and Greek. Its proof, Matthew 5:18,
   is in the Greek a saying about the law's authority. The Second London
   Confession keeps the claim but replaces the proof with Romans 3:2, which
   is apt but promises custody, not purity. The Helvetic Consensus, not in
   the repository, goes furthest beyond Scripture.
3. **The Lutheran confessions fall short only in what they leave unsaid.**
   They never state that God had his word written so that it would be kept,
   though Scripture says so repeatedly.
4. **The confessions under-use the passages Scripture itself puts first.**
   Isaiah 40:8, 1 Peter 1:25, Matthew 24:35, Isaiah 59:21 and Deuteronomy
   31:21 would ground a doctrine of preservation more securely than Matthew
   5:18, and the confessions either do not cite them or cite them for
   something else.
5. **None of the confessions in the repository reckons with the passages
   that record loss.** Scripture records prophetic and apostolic writings
   that did not survive and a book of the law neglected in the temple. A
   claim that the word was "kept pure in all ages" is consonant with these
   if it is read of the word's substance, as God's word; read of every
   writing or every letter, it is not.
