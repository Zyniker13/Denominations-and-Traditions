# The Hebrew and the Greek of the Old Testament Preservation Passages

Part of [The Preservation of Scripture in Scripture and in the Confessions](README.md).

Every Old Testament passage in the collection is printed in
[passages.md](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md) in the Hebrew of the
Masoretic Text (MT, Westminster Leningrad Codex) and the Greek of the
Septuagint (LXX, Brenton's edition), each with an English translation made
from it. This page gathers the differences between the two, passage by
passage, and asks which of them changes what the passage claims.

Whether the preservation claims are better grounded in the Hebrew or in the
Greek *as texts*, in light of the history of each, is the subject of the
companion study
[preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md). This
page is narrower: it compares what the two texts *say* in these passages.

**[context]** marks a statement resting on history outside the texts;
**[inferred]** marks this study's own reading.

## Summary

Of the 48 Old Testament passages in the collection:

- **16** differ in a way that changes what is claimed, or whether the claim
  is there at all.
- **15** differ in wording only.
- **17** show no difference that matters.

**[inferred]** The differences do not run in one direction. The Hebrew alone
contains three of the collection's preservation statements (Psalm 138:2's
"thou hast magnified thy word", Isaiah 34:16's "book of the LORD", and, on
one reading, Psalm 12:7's "thou shalt preserve them"), and the fuller form of
a fourth (Isaiah 59:21's "thy seed's seed"). It also alone contains the
charge that Israel "perverted the words of the living God" (Jeremiah 23:36).
The Greek alone makes the Old Testament's permanence language uniform, with
one verb, "abides" (μένει), for several Hebrew ones, and it is the form in
which the New Testament quotes the central passage, Isaiah 40:6–8. Neither
text lacks the core claim that God's word endures.

## Differences that change the claim

| Passage | Hebrew (MT) | Greek (LXX) | Effect on the claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Psalm 12:6–7](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-12-6) (MT 12:7–8; LXX 11:7–8) | "Thou wilt keep **them**, thou wilt preserve **him**" | "Thou shalt keep **us**, and preserve **us**" | In the Greek the verse is about the preservation of the faithful, not of words. In the Hebrew the referent is disputed; the grammar favours the poor of v. 5. |
| [Psalm 138:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-138-2) (LXX 137:2) | "Thou hast magnified **thy word** above all thy name" | "Thou hast magnified **thy holy name** above every thing" | The exaltation of God's word is in the Hebrew only. |
| [Psalm 145:13](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#ps-145-13) (LXX 144:13) | The line "The LORD is faithful in all his words" is **missing** | Missing in Brenton's text too; **[context]** present in other Greek manuscripts and in the Qumran Psalms Scroll (11QPs^a) | A claim of the faithfulness of God's words is itself unevenly transmitted. |
| [Isaiah 34:16](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-34-16) | "Seek ye out of the **book of the LORD**, and read: no one of these shall fail" | "They passed by in number, and not one of them perished" | The appeal to a written book is in the Hebrew only. |
| [Isaiah 8:16, 20](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-8-16) | "Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples … To the law and to the testimony" | "Those who seal up the law that they may not learn it" … "he has given the law for a help" | The command to seal the testimony among disciples, and the appeal "to the law and to the testimony" that Westminster cites, are in the Hebrew only. |
| [Isaiah 40:6–8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-40-8) | Longer: includes v. 7, "because the spirit of the LORD bloweth upon it: surely the people is grass"; "all its **goodliness**"; the word "shall **stand**" | Shorter, without most of v. 7; "all the **glory of man**"; the word "**abides**" | The core claim is identical. 1 Peter 1:24–25 quotes the Greek form. |
| [Isaiah 59:21](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-59-21) | The words shall not depart from thy mouth, "nor out of the mouth of thy seed, **nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed**" | Lacks "thy seed's seed"; singular verb | The Hebrew extends the promise explicitly to a third generation and beyond. |
| [Jeremiah 23:28–29, 36](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-23-28) | 23:36 ends "for ye have **perverted the words of the living God**" | Lacks the clause; adds in 23:28 "so are my words" | The charge of perverting God's words is in the Hebrew only. **[context]** The Greek Jeremiah represents a shorter Hebrew edition attested at Qumran. |
| [Jeremiah 8:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-8-8) | "the **lying pen** of the scribes has wrought falsely" | "In vain have the scribes used a false pen (reed)" | Both admit scribal falsehood; the Hebrew's charge is the sharper. |
| [Deuteronomy 10:2](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#exod-34-1) | "**I** will write" | "**Thou** shalt write" | Who rewrites the broken tables. Both agree in 10:4 that God wrote. |
| [Numbers 23:19](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#num-23-19) | "not a man, that he should **lie** … shall he not **make it stand**?" | "not as man to **waver** … shall he not **abide by** it?" | The Greek frames God's reliability as constancy rather than truthfulness. |
| [Zechariah 1:5–6](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#zech-1-5) | "My words … **did they not overtake** your fathers?" | "**Receive ye** my words … which I command **by my Spirit**" | The Hebrew claims the words outlived the prophets and came to pass; the Greek turns this into a command. |
| [Deuteronomy 8:3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#deut-8-3) | "by **everything** that proceeds from the mouth of the LORD" | "by every **word** that proceeds through the mouth of God" | The Greek supplies "word"; Matthew 4:4 follows it. |
| [Isaiah 30:8](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#isa-30-8) | לָעַד "**for ever**", as pointed; the same consonants read לְעֵד mean "**for a witness**" | "for many days, and even for ever" | The duration of the written witness turns on the vowels. |
| [Habakkuk 2:3](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#hab-2-2) | The vision "will not **lie**", it will surely come | "not in vain"; "**the coming one** will come" | The Greek can be read of a person, as Hebrews 10:37 reads it. |
| [Jeremiah 36:32](../../scripture/preservation/passages.md#jer-36) (LXX 43:32) | **Jeremiah** took another scroll and gave it to Baruch | **Baruch** took another scroll | Who restored the burned text. |

Two of these carry a note on the Hebrew text itself. In Deuteronomy 29:29
(MT 29:28) the words "unto us and to our children" bear the Masoretes'
*puncta extraordinaria*, dots marking letters as doubtful, in the very verse
that says the revealed things belong to Israel's children for ever. In
Exodus 34:1 the Greek adds "and come up to me to the mountain", from the next
verse.

## Patterns

1. **The Greek is sometimes shorter, and the shorter text lacks a
   preservation or corruption statement.** Isaiah 34:16 (the book), Isaiah
   40:7, Isaiah 59:21 (the third generation), Jeremiah 23:36 (the perversion
   of God's words). **[context]** In Jeremiah, and in places elsewhere, the
   Qumran scrolls show the Greek translating a shorter Hebrew text rather
   than abridging; the difference is then between two Hebrew editions (see
   [preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md §4.2](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#42-before-the-masoretes-several-hebrew-editions)).
2. **The Greek sometimes reads the passage differently.** Psalm 12:7, Psalm
   138:2, Isaiah 8:16, 20, Zechariah 1:6. In each, the Hebrew has the
   stronger statement about the word.
3. **The Greek unifies the vocabulary of permanence.** Four Hebrew
   expressions become "abides" (μένει, διαμένει); see
   [greek-consistency.md §1](greek-consistency.md#1-abides-for-ever-μένω-and-διαμένω).
4. **Some readings depend on vowels.** Isaiah 30:8 ("for ever" or "for a
   witness") and the suffixes of Psalm 12:7 rest on the Masoretic pointing or
   on the reading tradition behind it. **[inferred]** The consonantal text
   alone does not settle them.
5. **The New Testament quotes these passages from the Greek.** Isaiah 40:6–8
   (1 Peter 1:24–25), Deuteronomy 8:3 (Matthew 4:4), Habakkuk 2:3 (Hebrews
   10:37) and the covenant clause of Isaiah 59:21 (Romans 11:27) are all
   quoted in their Septuagint form.

## Which text makes the stronger claim?

**[inferred]** Passage by passage:

| Stronger in the Hebrew | Stronger in the Greek | Equal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psalm 12:7 (on the reading that "them" is the words); Psalm 138:2; Isaiah 8:16, 20; Isaiah 34:16; Isaiah 59:21; Zechariah 1:6; Isaiah 30:8 (as pointed, "for ever") | Numbers 23:19 ("abide by"); the uniform "abides" of the Psalms and Isaiah | Isaiah 40:8 and all the rest |

The Hebrew thus has somewhat more, and somewhat stronger, statements about
God's word being kept and written, and it also has the sharper admissions
that men falsified and perverted it (Jeremiah 8:8; 23:36). The Greek states
the permanence of the word in a single, uniform idiom, and it is the form
the New Testament uses. The central claim, that the word of God abides for
ever (Isaiah 40:8), is the same in both. Whether one text is the better
*ground* for these claims, given the history of each, is weighed in
[preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md](../preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md).
