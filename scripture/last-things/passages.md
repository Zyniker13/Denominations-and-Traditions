## The heavens and the “third heaven”

### 2 Corinthians 12:2–4

<a id="2cor-12-2-4"></a>

**Claim.** Paul knows a man in Christ who was caught up "as far as the third heaven", and again "into paradise", where he heard unspeakable words. *(Also: The final destination of the Christian.)*

**Greek (Textus Receptus)**

> 2 Corinthians 12:2 — Οἶδα ἄνθρωπον ἐν Χριστῷ πρὸ ἐτῶν δεκατεσσάρων — εἴτε ἐν σώματι οὐκ οἶδα· εἴτε ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος οὐκ οἶδα· ὁ Θεὸς οἶδεν — ἁρπαγέντα τὸν τοιοῦτον ἕως τρίτου οὐρανοῦ.
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:3 — Καὶ οἶδα τὸν τοιοῦτον ἄνθρωπον — εἴτε ἐν σώματι, εἴτε ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος, οὐκ οἶδα· ὁ Θεὸς οἶδεν —
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:4 — ὅτι ἡρπάγη εἰς τὸν παράδεισον, καὶ ἤκουσεν ἄρρητα ῥήματα, ἃ οὐκ ἐξὸν ἀνθρώπῳ λαλῆσαι.

**Greek (Byzantine, 1904 Patriarchal text)**

> 2 Corinthians 12:2 — οἶδα ἄνθρωπον ἐν Χριστῷ πρὸ ἐτῶν δεκατεσσάρων· εἴτε ἐν σώματι οὐκ οἶδα, εἴτε ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος οὐκ οἶδα, ὁ Θεὸς οἶδεν· ἁρπαγέντα τὸν τοιοῦτον ἕως τρίτου οὐρανοῦ.
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:3 — καὶ οἶδα τὸν τοιοῦτον ἄνθρωπον· εἴτε ἐν σώματι εἴτε ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος οὐκ οἶδα, ὁ Θεὸς οἶδεν·
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:4 — ὅτι ἡρπάγη εἰς τὸν παράδεισον καὶ ἤκουσεν ἄρρητα ῥήματα, ἃ οὐκ ἐξὸν ἀνθρώπῳ λαλῆσαι.

**Greek (SBL Greek New Testament, CC BY 4.0)**

> 2 Corinthians 12:2 — οἶδα ἄνθρωπον ἐν Χριστῷ πρὸ ἐτῶν δεκατεσσάρων— εἴτε ἐν σώματι οὐκ οἶδα, εἴτε ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος οὐκ οἶδα, ὁ θεὸς οἶδεν— ἁρπαγέντα τὸν τοιοῦτον ἕως τρίτου οὐρανοῦ.
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:3 — καὶ οἶδα τὸν τοιοῦτον ἄνθρωπον— εἴτε ἐν σώματι εἴτε χωρὶς τοῦ σώματος οὐκ οἶδα, ὁ θεὸς οἶδεν—
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:4 — ὅτι ἡρπάγη εἰς τὸν παράδεισον καὶ ἤκουσεν ἄρρητα ῥήματα ἃ οὐκ ἐξὸν ἀνθρώπῳ λαλῆσαι.

**English (KJV, 1769)**

> 2 Corinthians 12:2 — I knew a man in Christ above fourteen years ago, (whether in the body, I cannot tell; or whether out of the body, I cannot tell: God knoweth;) such an one caught up to the third heaven.
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:3 — And I knew such a man, (whether in the body, or out of the body, I cannot tell: God knoweth;)
>
> 2 Corinthians 12:4 — How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter.

**The Greek editions compared.**

In verse 3 the Textus Receptus and the Byzantine text read ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος ("out of the body", as in verse 2), the SBL text χωρὶς τοῦ σώματος ("apart from the body"). The sense is the same; nothing in the editions bears on the number of heavens.

**The words.**

ἁρπάζω is to seize and carry off (the same verb in 1 Thess. 4:17, "caught up ... to meet the Lord in the air"). ἕως τρίτου οὐρανοῦ is "up to, as far as a third heaven": the ordinal implies a series counted from below but does not say how many there are. τρίτου οὐρανοῦ has no article. παράδεισος is the Septuagint's word for the garden of Eden (Gen. 2:8, παράδεισον ἐν Ἐδέμ, for Hebrew גַּן־בְּעֵדֶן); it is used of the place of the blessed dead in Luke 23:43 and of the place of the tree of life in Rev. 2:7. Verses 3–4 repeat the sentence of verse 2 with παράδεισον in place of τρίτου οὐρανοῦ; whether this is one rapture told twice or two raptures is not settled by the grammar.

**Notes.**

The Reformed reading (Calvin on this verse; Poole; Hodge) is that "the third heaven" is the highest heaven, the dwelling of God and the blessed, counted after the heaven of the air and the heaven of the stars, and that "paradise" names the same place. R. H. Charles (1896) read the verse against the seven heavens of 2 Enoch and the Testament of Levi, in which paradise is placed in the third heaven, and concluded that Paul held seven heavens. The Talmud knows two heavens (R. Judah) or seven (Resh Lakish), Hagigah 12b; it puts the souls of the righteous in the seventh, ʿaravot. See analysis/heaven-and-resurrection/third-heaven.md.

### Deuteronomy 10:14

<a id="deut-10-14"></a>

**Claim.** The heaven and the heaven of heavens belong to the LORD, with the earth and all that is in it.

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Deuteronomy 10:14 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">הֵן לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ הַשָּׁמַיִם וּשְׁמֵי הַשָּׁמָיִם הָאָרֶץ וְכָל־אֲשֶׁר־בָּֽהּ ׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Deuteronomy 10:14 — Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens [is] the LORD’s thy God, the earth [also], with all that therein [is].

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Deuteronomy 10:14 — Ἰδοὺ Κυρίου τοῦ Θεοῦ σου ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ὁ οὐρανὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ, ἡ γῆ καὶ πάντα ὅσα ἐστὶν ἐν αὐτῇ.

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

> Deuteronomy 10:14 — ἰδοὺ Κυρίου τοῦ θεοῦ σου ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ὁ οὐρανὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ, ἡ γῆ καὶ πάντα ὅσα ἐστὶν ἐν αὐτῇ·

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Deuteronomy 10:14 — Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens belong to the Lord thy God, the earth and all things that are in it.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew שְׁמֵי הַשָּׁמַיִם, a construct chain ("the heavens of the heavens"), is the Hebrew superlative: the highest heavens. The Greek renders it in the singular, ὁ οὐρανὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ. Neither text numbers the heavens.

**Notes.**

The Old Testament phrase behind the Reformed scheme of a highest heaven above the visible heavens (the "heaven of heavens", caelum empyreum).

### 1 Kings 8:27

<a id="1kgs-8-27"></a>

**Claim.** Solomon: the heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain God; how much less the house he has built.

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> 1 Kings 8:27 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כִּי הַֽאֻמְנָם יֵשֵׁב אֱלֹהִים עַל־הָאָרֶץ הִנֵּה הַשָּׁמַיִם וּשְׁמֵי הַשָּׁמַיִם לֹא יְכַלְכְּלוּךָ אַף כִּֽי־הַבַּיִת הַזֶּה אֲשֶׁר בָּנִֽיתִי׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> 1 Kings 8:27 — But will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded?

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> 1 Kings 8:27 — Ὅτι εἰ ἀληθῶς κατοικήσει ὁ Θεὸς μετὰ ἀνθρώπων ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς; εἰ ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ὁ οὐρανὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ οὐκ ἀρκέσουσί σοι, πλὴν καὶ ὁ οἶκος οὗτος ὃν ᾠκοδόμησα τῷ ὀνόματί σου;

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

> 1 Kings 8:27 — ὅτι εἰ ἀληθῶς κατοικήσει ὁ θεὸς μετὰ ἀνθρώπων ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς; εἰ ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ὁ οὐρανὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ οὐκ ἀρκέσουσίν σοι, πλὴν καὶ ὁ οἶκος οὗτος ὃν ᾠκοδόμησα τῷ ὀνόματί σου;

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> 1 Kings 8:27 — But will God indeed dwell with men upon the earth? if the heaven and heaven of heavens will not suffice thee, how much less even this house which I have built to thy name?

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew says the heavens "cannot contain" (כּוּל, Pilpel) God; the Greek says they "will not suffice" (ἀρκέω) for him. Both deny that any heaven, the highest included, circumscribes God.

**Notes.**

Reformed and Lutheran writers agree that the heaven of the blessed is a created place and that God is not contained by it. The Lutherans (Schmid's Gerhard and Quenstedt) used the verse with their teaching on the right hand of God, which is not a place; the Reformed held that Christ's human body is in a place, the highest heaven, and not everywhere. **English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

In the Greek the verse belongs to 2 Esdras (19:6), and the speaker is named: καὶ εἶπεν Ἔσδρας. The heaven of heavens is again singular in Greek.

### Luke 23:43

<a id="luke-23-43"></a>

**Claim.** "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise." *(Also: The final destination of the Christian.)*

**Greek (Textus Receptus)**

> Luke 23:43 — Καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς, Ἀμὴν λέγω σοι, σήμερον μετ᾿ ἐμοῦ ἔσῃ ἐν τῷ παραδείσῳ.

**Greek (Byzantine, 1904 Patriarchal text)**

> Luke 23:43 — καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς· ἀμὴν λέγω σοι, σήμερον μετ᾽ ἐμοῦ ἔσῃ ἐν τῷ παραδείσῳ.

**Greek (SBL Greek New Testament, CC BY 4.0)**

> Luke 23:43 — καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῷ Ἀμήν σοι λέγω σήμερον μετʼ ἐμοῦ ἔσῃ ἐν τῷ παραδείσῳ.

**English (KJV, 1769)**

> Luke 23:43 — And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.

**The words.**

The comma after σοι is the editors'. The reading "I say to thee today, thou shalt be" takes σήμερον with λέγω. Luke has the formula ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν five other times (4:24; 12:37; 18:17, 29; 21:32), and each time it opens what follows with nothing added to λέγω; the SBL text has ἀμήν σοι λέγω.

**Notes.**

The proof text of the Reformed (Westminster Confession 32.1; Calvin, Institutes 3.25.6) and the Lutherans alike that the souls of believers go at death into the presence of Christ, without an intermediate sleep.

**The Greek editions compared.**

The Textus Receptus adds εἰς τὸ γενέσθαι αὐτὸ ("that it may become") before σύμμορφον; the Byzantine and SBL texts lack it, and read αὐτῷ for ἑαυτῷ at the end of the verse. The sense is unchanged.

The Saviour is awaited ἐξ οὗ, "from" heaven. μετασχηματίζω is to change the σχῆμα, the outward fashion; σύμμορφος, "of the same μορφή". The subject of the change is τὸ σῶμα τῆς ταπεινώσεως ἡμῶν, the same body.

**Notes.**

Calvin and the Reformed: the same body changed in quality, not exchanged for another. The Raavad made the same point against Maimonides from the Talmud: the sages did not teach "that there is no resurrection to body but the souls alone" (theologians/jewish-medieval/maimonides/); Paul grounds it in the body of the risen Christ.

**The Greek editions compared.**

In verse 2 the Byzantine and SBL texts lack ἐγὼ Ἰωάννης. In verse 3 the voice is ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ in the Textus Receptus and the Byzantine text, ἐκ τοῦ θρόνου in the SBL text; and the Textus Receptus alone ends with Θεὸς αὐτῶν, "(and be) their God".

**Notes.**

The text on which a final destination on the renewed earth is argued. ## The new heavens and the new earth

### Isaiah 65:17

<a id="isa-65-17"></a>

**Claim.** "Behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered."

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 65:17 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כִּֽי־הִנְנִי בוֹרֵא שָׁמַיִם חֲדָשִׁים וָאָרֶץ חֲדָשָׁה וְלֹא תִזָּכַרְנָה הָרִאשֹׁנוֹת וְלֹא תַעֲלֶינָה עַל־לֵֽב׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 65:17 — ¶ For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 65:17 — Ἔσται γὰρ ὁ οὐρανὸς καινὸς, καὶ ἡ γῆ καινὴ, καὶ οὐ μὴ μνησθῶσι τῶν προτέρων, οὐδʼ οὐ μὴ ἐπέλθῃ αὐτῶν ἐπὶ τὴν καρδίαν,

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

> Isaiah 65:17 — Ἔσται γὰρ ὁ οὐρανὸς καινὸς καὶ ἡ γῆ καινή, καὶ οὐ μὴ μνησθῶσιν τῶν προτέρων οὐδʼ οὐ μὴ ἐπέλθῃ αὐτῶν ἐπὶ τὴν καρδίαν,

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 65:17 — For there shall be a new heaven and a new earth: and they shall not at all remember the former, neither shall they at all come into their mind.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew is a participle, בוֹרֵא, "I am creating", with בָּרָא, the verb of Gen. 1:1. The Greek has no verb of creating: ἔσται γὰρ ὁ οὐρανὸς καινός, "for the heaven shall be new", singular. The Hebrew חָדָשׁ, like Greek καινός, is "new" in kind and freshness; the Greek νέος, "new in time", is not used here or in the New Testament passages.

**Notes.**

Calvin applies the verse first to the restored church, its full accomplishment at the last day; Owen's sermon of 1649 reads the new heavens and earth as the restored condition of the Church; Maimonides (Guide II.29) takes it as a figure for Israel's restoration in a world that will never pass away; the Raavad, against him, holds that God "will renew His world" after a thousand years of desolation (Sanhedrin 97a).

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

### Isaiah 51:6

<a id="isa-51-6"></a>

**Claim.** The heavens shall vanish away like smoke, the earth wax old like a garment, but God's salvation shall be for ever.

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

> Isaiah 51:6 — ἄρατε εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς ὑμῶν, καὶ ἐμβλέψατε εἰς τὴν γῆν κάτω, ὅτι ὁ οὐρανὸς ὡς καπνὸς ἐστερεώθη, ἡ δὲ γῆ ὡς ἱμάτιον παλαιωθήσεται, οἱ δὲ κατοικοῦντες ὥσπερ ταῦτα ἀποθανοῦνται, τὸ δὲ σωτήριόν μου εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα ἔσται, ἡ δὲ δικαιοσύνη μου οὐ μὴ ἐκλίπῃ.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew נִמְלָחוּ, found only here in this sense, is taken as "be dissipated, vanish" (KJV "vanish away"). With Ps. 102:26–27 the strongest Old Testament language for the passing of the present heavens; the Reformed read "wax old like a garment" of change of state, the Lutherans of the end of the substance.

*Swete and Göttingen differ here:* Swete κατʼ ἀρχὰς τὴν γῆν σύ, Κύριε, ἐθεμελίωσας, Göttingen κατʼ ἀρχὰς σύ, Κύριε, τὴν γῆν ἐθεμελίωσας; Swete ἑλίξεις αὐτοὺς, Göttingen ἀλλάξεις αὐτοὺς.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew יֹאבֵדוּ, "they shall perish", is followed by תַּחֲלִיפֵם וְיַחֲלֹפוּ, "thou shalt change them and they shall be changed" (חָלַף, to change, pass on, put on a fresh garment). The Greek has ἀπολοῦνται and ἀλλαγήσονται; for the Hebrew "thou shalt change them" it has ἑλίξεις αὐτούς, "thou shalt roll them up", in Brenton's and Swete's texts; the Göttingen edition reads ἀλλάξεις, "thou shalt change", with the Hebrew. The Hebrew numbers the verses 26–28 (the title is verse 1).

**Notes.**

The Lutheran dogmaticians read "perish" (ἀπολοῦνται) of the destruction of the substance; the Reformed read the clause that follows, "changed as a vesture" (חָלַף; ἀλλάσσω), of a change of the same heavens. The verse holds both words.

The reading εὑρεθήσεται, which none of these writers discusses, sits more easily with renewal than κατακαήσεται does. [inferred]

## The resurrection of the body

### Job 19:25–27

<a id="job-19-25-27"></a>

**Claim.** "I know that my redeemer liveth ... and though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God: whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another."

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Job 19:25 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַאֲנִי יָדַעְתִּי גֹּאֲלִי חָי וְאַחֲרוֹן עַל־עָפָר יָקֽוּם׃</span>
>
> Job 19:26 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וְאַחַר עוֹרִֽי נִקְּפוּ־זֹאת וּמִבְּשָׂרִי אֶֽחֱזֶה אֱלֽוֹהַּ׃</span>
>
> Job 19:27 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֲשֶׁר אֲנִי ׀ אֶֽחֱזֶה־לִּי וְעֵינַי רָאוּ וְלֹא־זָר כָּלוּ כִלְיֹתַי בְּחֵקִֽי׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Job 19:25 — For I know [that] my redeemer liveth, and [that] he shall stand at the latter [day] upon the earth:
>
> Job 19:26 — And [though] after my skin [worms] destroy this [body], yet in my flesh shall I see God:
>
> Job 19:27 — Whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another; [though] my reins be consumed within me.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Job 19:25 — Οἶδα γὰρ ὅτι ἀένναός ἐστιν ὁ ἐκλύειν με μέλλων,
>
> Job 19:26 — ἐπὶ γῆς ἀναστῆσαι τὸ δέρμα μου τὸ ἀναντλοῦν ταῦτα· παρὰ γὰρ Κυρίου ταῦτά μοι συνετελέσθη,
>
> Job 19:27 — ἃ ἐγὼ ἐμαυτῷ συνεπίσταμαι, ἃ ὁ ὀφθαλμός μου ἑώρακε, καὶ οὐκ ἄλλος, πάντα δέ μοι συντετέλεσται ἐν κόλπῳ.

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

> Job 19:25–27 — οἶδα γὰρ ὅτι ἀέναός ἐστιν ὁ ἐκλύειν με μέλλων· ἐπὶ γῆς ἀναστήσαι τὸ δέρμα μου τὸ ἀναντλοῦν ταῦτα. παρὰ γὰρ Κυρίου ταῦτά μοι συνετελέσθη, ἃ ἐγὼ ἐμαυτῷ συνεπίσταμαι, ἃ ὁ ὀφθαλμός μου ἑόρακεν καὶ οὐκ ἄλλος· πάντα δέ μοι συντετέλεσται ἐν κόλπῳ.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Job 19:25 — For I know that he is eternal who is about to deliver me,
>
> Job 19:26 — [and] to raise up upon the earth my skin that endures these [sufferings]: for these things have been accomplished to me of the Lord;
>
> Job 19:27 — which I am conscious of in myself, which mine eye has seen, and not another, but all have been fulfilled to me in [my] bosom.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew is hard. מִבְּשָׂרִי is "from my flesh": either "out of my flesh", in it and looking out of it (KJV "in my flesh"), or "away from my flesh", without it. וְעֵינַי רָאוּ וְלֹא־זָר, "my eyes shall see, and not a stranger", stresses the identity of the one who sees. The Greek is a different text: "for I know that he is eternal who is about to deliver me, to raise up (ἀναστῆσαι) upon the earth my skin that endures these things"; it has no "in my flesh" and no seeing of God, but has the verb of resurrection.

**Notes.**

Calvin's sermon on Job 19 and the Reformed writers take the passage as a plain confession of the resurrection of the same flesh. The Hebrew allows it and does not compel it; the Greek, which the Reformed did not argue from, states resurrection outright.

### Isaiah 26:19

<a id="isa-26-19"></a>

**Claim.** "Thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust ... the earth shall cast out the dead."

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 26:19 — Thy dead [men] shall live, [together with] my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust: for thy dew [is as] the dew of herbs, and the earth shall cast out the dead.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 26:19 — Ἀναστήσονται οἱ νεκροὶ, καὶ ἐγερθήσονται οἱ ἐν τοῖς μνημείοις, καὶ εὐφρανθήσονται οἱ ἐν τῇ γῇ· ἡ γὰρ δρόσος ἡ παρὰ σοῦ ἴαμα αὐτοῖς ἐστιν, ἡ δὲ γῆ τῶν ἀσεβῶν πεσεῖται.

**Greek (Septuagint, Swete's edition)**

> Isaiah 26:19 — ἀναστήσονται οἱ νεκροί, καὶ ἐγερθήσονται οἱ ἐν τοῖς μνημείοις, καὶ εὐφρανθήσονται οἱ ἐν τῇ γῇ· ἡ γὰρ δρόσος ἡ παρὰ σοῦ ἴαμα αὐτοῖς ἐστιν, ἡ δὲ γῆ τῶν ἀσεβῶν πεσεῖται.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 26:19 — The dead shall rise, and they that are in the tombs shall be raised, and they that are in the earth shall rejoice: for the dew from thee is healing to them: but the land of the ungodly shall perish.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

נְבֵלָתִי is "my corpse" (singular with suffix); the KJV supplies "together with". Kohler (1918) would read "thy dead" for "my dead bodies", and take אוֹרֹת as "herbs" (as in 2 Kings 4:39), the dew being a "dew of revival", "also a rabbinic term". The Greek is plainer: "the dead shall rise, and those in the tombs shall be raised", and the dew is "healing to them". In the Talmud R. Gamaliel cites this verse as the Prophets' proof of the resurrection (Sanhedrin 90b), and the dew "with which the Holy One ... is about to quicken mortals" is kept in ʿaravot, the seventh heaven (Hagigah 12b).

> 1 Corinthians 15:41 — [There is] one glory of the sun, and another glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars: for [one] star differeth from [another] star in glory.
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:42 — So also [is] the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption:
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:43 — It is sown in dishonour; it is raised in glory: it is sown in weakness; it is raised in power:
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:44 — It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body. There is a natural body, and there is a spiritual body.

**The Greek editions compared.**

In verse 44 the Textus Receptus reads ἔστι σῶμα ψυχικόν, καὶ ἔστι σῶμα πνευματικόν ("there is a natural body, and there is a spiritual body"), the SBL text εἰ ἔστιν σῶμα ψυχικόν, ἔστιν καὶ πνευματικόν ("if there is a natural body, there is also a spiritual"). In verse 39 the order of birds and fishes differs.

**The words.**

ψυχικός is "of the ψυχή", animated by the soul (the adjective of Gen. 2:7 LXX, εἰς ψυχὴν ζῶσαν, quoted in v. 45); πνευματικός is "of the πνεῦμα", governed by the Spirit. Both qualify σῶμα: the contrast is between two bodies, not between body and spirit. The subject of the four verbs σπείρεται ... ἐγείρεται is unexpressed and the same. "Not the body that shall be" (v. 37) and "bare grain" (γυμνὸν κόκκον, literally "naked grain") are the Talmud's image too: R. Meir argues from wheat "buried naked" that comes out "dressed in so many garments" (Sanhedrin 90b).

**Notes.**

Calvin: the same body raised with a new quality; the "spiritual body" is not a body of spiritual substance. The Lutheran dogmaticians teach the same numerical identity. 2 Baruch 49–51 asks Paul's question in the same words.

**English (KJV, 1769)**

> 1 Corinthians 15:50 — Now this I say, brethren, that flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God; neither doth corruption inherit incorruption.
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:51 — Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed,
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:52 — In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:53 — For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal [must] put on immortality.
>
> 1 Corinthians 15:54 — So when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory.

**The words.**

σὰρξ καὶ αἷμα is a Hebrew idiom (בָּשָׂר וָדָם) for man in his mortal frailty (Matt. 16:17; Gal. 1:16), explained by the next clause, "neither doth corruption inherit incorruption". τὸ φθαρτὸν τοῦτο, "this corruptible", with the demonstrative, is what puts on incorruption: the same body. ἐνδύσασθαι, "put on", is the clothing language of 2 Cor. 5:2–4.

**Notes.**

"Flesh and blood" is read by Calvin and the Reformed of the quality, not the substance, of the flesh; by Hodge of the present organization of the body, "not adapted to our future state of being".
