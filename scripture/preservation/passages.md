# The preservation passages

Every passage of Scripture in which a claim is made about God's Word
enduring, not passing away, not failing, being kept, or not being altered;
with the passages that describe how it was kept, and those that admit its
corruption or withdrawal among men. The study of them is in
[analysis/preservation-of-scripture/](../../analysis/preservation-of-scripture/README.md).

> **Generated** by `tools/build_preservation.py` from
> [passages.yaml](passages.yaml). Edit that file and re-run the tool; do not
> edit this one. The same data, with every verse, is in
> [passages.json](passages.json).

For each Old Testament passage the Hebrew (Masoretic Text) and the Greek
(Septuagint) are always given, each with an English translation made from
it: the King James Version for the Hebrew and Brenton's translation for the
Greek. For each New Testament passage three Greek editions are given: the
Textus Receptus, the Byzantine text of 1904, and the SBL Greek New Testament
(a modern critical text). The editions and their licenses are listed in
[README.md](README.md#editions) and [SOURCES.md](../../SOURCES.md).

The Septuagint is cited by Brenton's chapter and verse, which often differ
from the Hebrew (most Psalms are one lower; Jeremiah 25–51 is arranged
differently). The Hebrew is cited by its own numbering, which sometimes
differs from the English (the Psalm titles are counted as verses).

## Old Testament

### Exodus 17:14

<a id="exod-17-14"></a>

**Claim.** God commands Moses to write a memorial "in a book" (בַּסֵּפֶר) and rehearse it to Joshua.

**Speaker:** God · **Role:** means · **Categories:** Inscripturation · **Object:** a record of the war with Amalek

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Exodus 17:14 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל־מֹשֶׁה כְּתֹב זֹאת זִכָּרוֹן בַּסֵּפֶר וְשִׂים בְּאָזְנֵי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ כִּֽי־מָחֹה אֶמְחֶה אֶת־זֵכֶר עֲמָלֵק מִתַּחַת הַשָּׁמָֽיִם׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Exodus 17:14 — And the LORD said unto Moses, Write this [for] a memorial in a book, and rehearse [it] in the ears of Joshua: for I will utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Exodus 17:14 — Εἶπε δὲ Κύριος πρὸς Μωυσῆν, Κατάγραψον τοῦτο εἰς μνημόσυνον εἰς βιβλίον, καὶ δὸς εἰς τὰ ὦτα Ἰησοῖ· ὅτι ἀλοιφῇ ἐξαλείψω τὸ μνημόσυνον Ἀμαλὴκ ἐκ τῆς ὑπὸ τὸν οὐρανόν.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Exodus 17:14 — And the Lord said to Moses, Write this for a memorial in a book, and speak [this] in the ears of Joshua; for I will utterly blot out the memorial of Amalec from under heaven.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

Substantially the same. The Greek names Joshua Ἰησοῦς, the same name as Jesus.

**Notes.**

The first divine command to write in a book. Writing is given as the means of memory across generations.

### Exodus 34:1; Deuteronomy 10:2, 4

<a id="exod-34-1"></a>

**Claim.** After Moses breaks the first tables, God rewrites "the words that were in the first tables" on new ones, "according to the first writing."

**Speaker:** God · **Role:** means · **Categories:** Inscripturation, Restoration after loss · **Object:** the words on the first tables

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Exodus 34:1 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל־מֹשֶׁה פְּסָל־לְךָ שְׁנֵֽי־לֻחֹת אֲבָנִים כָּרִאשֹׁנִים וְכָתַבְתִּי עַל־הַלֻּחֹת אֶת־הַדְּבָרִים אֲשֶׁר הָיוּ עַל־הַלֻּחֹת הָרִאשֹׁנִים אֲשֶׁר שִׁבַּֽרְתָּ׃</span>
>
> Deuteronomy 10:2 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וְאֶכְתֹּב עַל־הַלֻּחֹת אֶת־הַדְּבָרִים אֲשֶׁר הָיוּ עַל־הַלֻּחֹת הָרִאשֹׁנִים אֲשֶׁר שִׁבַּרְתָּ וְשַׂמְתָּם בָּאָרֽוֹן׃</span>
>
> Deuteronomy 10:4 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיִּכְתֹּב עַֽל־הַלֻּחֹת כַּמִּכְתָּב הָרִאשׁוֹן אֵת עֲשֶׂרֶת הַדְּבָרִים אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֲלֵיכֶם בָּהָר מִתּוֹךְ הָאֵשׁ בְּיוֹם הַקָּהָל וַיִּתְּנֵם יְהוָה אֵלָֽי׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Exodus 34:1 — And the LORD said unto Moses, Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first: and I will write upon [these] tables the words that were in the first tables, which thou brakest.
>
> Deuteronomy 10:2 — And I will write on the tables the words that were in the first tables which thou brakest, and thou shalt put them in the ark.
>
> Deuteronomy 10:4 — And he wrote on the tables, according to the first writing, the ten commandments, which the LORD spake unto you in the mount out of the midst of the fire in the day of the assembly: and the LORD gave them unto me.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Exodus 34:1 — Καὶ εἶπε Κύριος πρὸς Μωυσῆν λαξευσον σεαυτῷ δύο πλάκας λιθίνας, καθὼς καὶ αἱ πρῶται, καὶ ἀνάβηθι πρὸς μὲ εἰς τὸ ὄρος, καὶ γράψω ἐπὶ τῶν πλακῶν τὰ ῥήματα ἃ ἦν ἐν ταῖς πλαξὶ ταῖς πρώταις, αἷς συνέτριψας.
>
> Deuteronomy 10:2 — Καὶ γράψεις ἐπὶ τὰς πλάκας τὰ ῥήματα, ἃ ἦν ἐν ταῖς πλαξὶν ταῖς πρώταις ἃς συνέτριψας, καὶ ἐμβαλεῖς αὐτὰ εἰς τὴν κιβωτόν.
>
> Deuteronomy 10:4 — Καὶ ἔγραψεν ἐπὶ τὰς πλάκας κατὰ τὴν γραφὴν τὴν πρώτην τοὺς δέκα λόγους, οὓς ἐλάλησε Κύριος πρὸς ὑμᾶς ἐν τῷ ὄρει ἐκ μέσου τοῦ πυρός, καὶ ἔδωκεν αὐτὰς Κύριος ἐμοί.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Exodus 34:1 — And the Lord said to Moses, Hew for thyself two tables of stone, as also the first were, and come up to me to the mountain; and I will write upon the tables the words, which were on the first tables, which thou brokest.
>
> Deuteronomy 10:2 — And thou shalt write upon the tables the words which were on the first tables which thou didst break, and thou shalt put them into the ark.
>
> Deuteronomy 10:4 — And he wrote upon the tables according to the first writing the ten commandments, which the Lord spoke to you in the mountain out of the midst of the fire, and the Lord gave them to me.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

In Deuteronomy 10:2 the Hebrew has God say "I will write" (וְאֶכְתֹּב); the
Greek has "thou shalt write" (καὶ γράψεις). Both agree in 10:4 that God
wrote. Exodus 34:1 in the Greek adds "and come up to me to the mountain"
(from 34:2 and Deut 10:1).

**Notes.**

The one narrative in which a destroyed autograph is replaced with an
identical text. The restoration is by God's own act, not by the
community's copying.

### Numbers 23:19

<a id="num-23-19"></a>

**Claim.** God is not a man that he should lie; what he has spoken he will make good.

**Speaker:** God, through a prophet · **Role:** ground · **Categories:** Fulfilment · **Object:** what God has said and spoken

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Numbers 23:19 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">לֹא אִישׁ אֵל וִֽיכַזֵּב וּבֶן־אָדָם וְיִתְנֶחָם הַהוּא אָמַר וְלֹא יַעֲשֶׂה וְדִבֶּר וְלֹא יְקִימֶֽנָּה׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Numbers 23:19 — God [is] not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do [it]? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Numbers 23:19 — Οὐχ ὡς ἄνθρωπος ὁ Θεὸς διαρτηθῆναι, οὐδʼ ὡς υἱὸς ἀνθρώπου ἀπειληθῆναι, αὐτὸς εἴπας, οὐχὶ ποιήσει; λαλήσει, καὶ οὐχὶ ἐμμενεῖ;

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Numbers 23:19 — God is not as man to waver, nor as the son of man to be threatened; shall he say and not perform? shall he speak and not keep [to his word]?

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew: "God is not a man, that he should lie (וִיכַזֵּב) … hath he
spoken, and shall he not make it stand (יְקִימֶנָּה)?" The Greek: "God is
not as man to waver (διαρτηθῆναι), nor as the son of man to be threatened
(ἀπειληθῆναι) … shall he speak and not abide by it (οὐχὶ ἐμμενεῖ)?" The
Greek turns "lie … repent" into "waver … be threatened", and "establish"
into "abide by".

**Notes.**

Balaam's oracle, given as the word God "put in his mouth" (23:16).

### Deuteronomy 4:2; 12:32

<a id="deut-4-2"></a>

**Claim.** Ye shall not add unto the word, neither diminish from it.

**Speaker:** Moses or a prophet, for God · **Role:** command · **Categories:** Prohibition of alteration · **Object:** the word Moses commands

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Deuteronomy 4:2 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">לֹא תֹסִפוּ עַל־הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם וְלֹא תִגְרְעוּ מִמֶּנּוּ לִשְׁמֹר אֶת־מִצְוֺת יְהוָה אֱלֹֽהֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶֽם׃</span>
>
> Deuteronomy 13:1 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֵת כָּל־הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם אֹתוֹ תִשְׁמְרוּ לַעֲשׂוֹת לֹא־תֹסֵף עָלָיו וְלֹא תִגְרַע מִמֶּֽנּוּ׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Deuteronomy 4:2 — Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish [ought] from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.
>
> Deuteronomy 12:32 — What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Deuteronomy 4:2 — Οὐ προσθήσετε πρὸς τὸ ῥῆμα ὃ ἐγὼ ἐντέλλομαι ὑμῖν, καὶ οὐκ ἀφελεῖτε ἀπʼ αὐτοῦ· φυλάσσεσθε τὰς ἐντολὰς Κυρίου τοῦ Θεοῦ ἡμῶν, ὅσα ἐγὼ ἐντέλλομαι ὑμῖν σήμερον.
>
> Deuteronomy 13:1 — Πᾶν ῥῆμα ὃ ἐγὼ ἐντλλομαι ὑμῖν σήμερον, τοῦτο φυλάξῃ ποιεῖν· οὐ προσθήσεις ἐπʼ αὐτό, οὐδὲ ἀφελεῖς ἀπʼ αὐτοῦ.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Deuteronomy 4:2 — Ye shall not add to the word which I command you, and ye shall not take from it: keep the commandments of the Lord our God, all that I command you this day.
>
> Deuteronomy 13:1 — Every word that I command you this day, it shalt thou observe to do: thou shalt not add to it, nor diminish from it.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The same command in both: לֹא תֹסִפוּ … וְלֹא תִגְרְעוּ / οὐ προσθήσετε …
οὐκ ἀφελεῖτε. In 4:2 the Greek reads "the Lord our God" for "the LORD your
God" and adds "this day" (σήμερον). Deuteronomy 12:32 in English Bibles is
13:1 in the Hebrew and the Greek.

**Used in the New Testament:** Revelation 22:18-19

**Notes.**

A command to Israel, not a promise. It presupposes that adding and taking
away are possible. The verbs recur in Revelation 22:18–19 (ἀφαιρέω in
both; the Greek of Deuteronomy uses προστίθημι for "add", Revelation
ἐπιτίθημι).

### Deuteronomy 8:3

<a id="deut-8-3"></a>

**Claim.** Man lives by every word that proceeds out of the mouth of the LORD.

**Speaker:** Moses or a prophet, for God · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Efficacy · **Object:** every word that proceeds from God's mouth

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Deuteronomy 8:3 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַֽיְעַנְּךָ וַיַּרְעִבֶךָ וַיַּֽאֲכִֽלְךָ אֶת הַמָּן אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יָדַעְתָּ וְלֹא יָדְעוּן אֲבֹתֶיךָ לְמַעַן הוֹדִֽעֲךָ כִּי לֹא עַל־הַלֶּחֶם לְבַדּוֹ יִחְיֶה הָֽאָדָם כִּי עַל־כָּל־מוֹצָא פִֽי־יְהוָה יִחְיֶה הָאָדָֽם׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Deuteronomy 8:3 — And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every [word] that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Deuteronomy 8:3 — Καὶ ἐκάκωσέ σε, καὶ ἐλιμαγχόνησέ σε, καὶ ἐψώμισέ σε τὸ μάννα, ὃ οὐκ ᾔδεισαν οἱ πατέρες σου· ἵνα ἀναγγείλῃ σοι, ὅτι οὐκ ἐπʼ ἄρτῳ μόνῳ ζήσεται ὁ ἄνθρωπος, ἀλλʼ ἐπὶ παντὶ ῥήματι τῷ ἐκπορευομένῳ διὰ στόματος Θεοῦ ζήσεται ὁ ἄνθρωπος.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Deuteronomy 8:3 — And he afflicted thee and straitened thee with hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thy fathers knew not; that he might teach thee that man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God shall man live.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew has no noun "word": "by everything that proceeds from the mouth
of the LORD" (כָּל־מוֹצָא פִי־יְהוָה). The Greek supplies one: "by every
word (παντὶ ῥήματι) that proceeds through the mouth of God." Jesus's
answer in Matthew 4:4 follows the Greek wording.

**Used in the New Testament:** Matthew 4:4, Luke 4:4

### Deuteronomy 18:18-19

<a id="deut-18-18"></a>

**Claim.** God will put his words in the mouth of a prophet like Moses, and require it of whoever will not hear them.

> Deuteronomy 29:29 — Τὰ κρυπτὰ Κυρίῳ τῷ Θεῷ ἡμῶν, τὰ δὲ φανερὰ ἡμῖν καὶ τοῖς τέκνοις ἡμῶν εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα, ποιεῖν πάντα τὰ ῥήματα τοῦ νόμου τούτου.

> Psalm 12:7 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אִֽמֲרוֹת יְהוָה אֲמָרוֹת טְהֹרוֹת כֶּסֶף צָרוּף בַּעֲלִיל לָאָרֶץ מְזֻקָּק שִׁבְעָתָֽיִם׃</span>
>
> Psalm 12:8 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אַתָּֽה־יְהוָה תִּשְׁמְרֵם תִּצְּרֶנּוּ ׀ מִן־הַדּוֹר זוּ לְעוֹלָֽם׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Psalm 12:6 — The words of the LORD [are] pure words: [as] silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times.
>
> Psalm 12:7 — Thou shalt keep them, O LORD, thou shalt preserve them from this generation for ever.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Psalm 11:7 — Τὰ λόγια Κυρίου, λόγια ἁγνά· ἀργύριον πεπυρωμένον, δοκίμιον τῇ γῇ, κεκαθαρισμένον ἑπταπλασίως.
>
> Psalm 11:8 — Σὺ Κύριε φυλάξεις ἡμᾶς· καὶ διατηρήσεις ἡμᾶς ἀπὸ τῆς γενεᾶς ταύτης, καὶ εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Psalm 11:7 — The oracles of the Lord are pure oracles; as silver tried in the fire, proved [in] a furnace of earth, purified seven times.
>
> Psalm 11:8 — Thou, O Lord, shalt keep us, and shalt preserve us, from this generation, and for ever.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The sharpest contrast in the collection.

- **Hebrew** (12:8): אַתָּה־יְהוָה תִּשְׁמְרֵם תִּצְּרֶנּוּ — "Thou, LORD,
  wilt keep *them*; thou wilt preserve *him* (or *it*)." The first suffix
  is masculine plural; the second is singular.
- **Greek** (11:8): Σὺ Κύριε φυλάξεις ἡμᾶς καὶ διατηρήσεις ἡμᾶς — "Thou,
  O Lord, shalt keep *us*, and shalt preserve *us*."

The Greek therefore makes the psalm a prayer for the preservation of the
faithful, not of the words. In the Hebrew, "words" (אִמֲרוֹת, v. 7) is
feminine plural, and the masculine "them" more naturally takes up "the
poor" and "the needy" of verse 6 (5 in English), whom God has just
promised to set in safety; but Hebrew does not always keep gender
agreement, which is why the verse has also been read of the words.
**[inferred]** The
grammar favours the poor; the Greek takes the same line, with a
first-person "us".

**Notes.**

The Westminster Larger Catechism (Q. 4) cites Psalm 12:6 for the
"purity" of Scripture, meaning its content, not its transmission. Verse 6
(7 in the Hebrew) itself is about purity, not preservation.

<a id="ps-145-13"></a>

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Psalm 145:13 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">מַֽלְכוּתְךָ מַלְכוּת כָּל־עֹֽלָמִים וּמֶֽמְשֶׁלְתְּךָ בְּכָל־דּוֹר וָדֽוֹר׃</span>

> Psalm 144:13 — Ἡ βασιλεία σου βασιλεία πάντων τῶν αἰώνων, καὶ ἡ δεσποτία σου ἐν πάσῃ γενεᾷ καὶ γενεᾷ·

texts are given to show the gap. The KJV, following the Hebrew, also
lacks it.

unevenly in the transmitted texts. The companion study

### Proverbs 30:5-6

<a id="prov-30-5"></a>

**Claim.** Every word of God is pure; add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.

**Speaker:** Sage · **Role:** command · **Categories:** Purity and truth, Prohibition of alteration · **Object:** every word of God

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Proverbs 30:5 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כָּל־אִמְרַת אֱלוֹהַּ צְרוּפָה מָגֵן הוּא לַֽחֹסִים בּֽוֹ׃</span>
>
> Proverbs 30:6 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אַל־תּוֹסְףְּ עַל־דְּבָרָיו פֶּן־יוֹכִיחַ בְּךָ וְנִכְזָֽבְתָּ׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Proverbs 30:5 — Every word of God [is] pure: he [is] a shield unto them that put their trust in him.
>
> Proverbs 30:6 — Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Proverbs 24:22k — Πάντες γὰρ λόγοι Θεοῦ πεπυρωμένοι, ὑπερασπίζει δὲ αὐτὸς τῶν εὐλαβουμένων αὐτόν.
>
> Proverbs 24:22l — Μὴ προσθῇς τοῖς λόγοις αὐτοῦ, ἵνα μὴ ἐλέγξῃ σε, καὶ ψευδὴς γένῃ.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Proverbs 24:22k — For all the words of God are tried in the fire, and he defends those that reverence him.
>
> Proverbs 24:22l — Add not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be made a liar.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The sense is the same ("all the words of God are tried", πάντες λόγοι
θεοῦ πεπυρωμένοι; "add not to his words", μὴ προσθῇς τοῖς λόγοις αὐτοῦ),
but the Greek places the words of Agur in chapter 24, where Brenton numbers
them 24:22k–l. The Hebrew "God" here is the rare אֱלוֹהַּ.

### Isaiah 8:16, 20

<a id="isa-8-16"></a>

**Claim.** Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples. To the law and to the testimony; if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them.

**Speaker:** God, through a prophet · **Role:** means · **Categories:** Custody · **Object:** the testimony and the law

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 8:16 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">צוֹר תְּעוּדָה חֲתוֹם תּוֹרָה בְּלִמֻּדָֽי׃</span>
>
> Isaiah 8:20 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">לְתוֹרָה וְלִתְעוּדָה אִם־לֹא יֹֽאמְרוּ כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה אֲשֶׁר אֵֽין־לוֹ שָֽׁחַר׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 8:16 — Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples.
>
> Isaiah 8:20 — To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, [it is] because [there is] no light in them.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 8:16 — Τότε φανεροὶ ἔσονται οἱ σφραγιζόμενοι τὸν νόμον τοῦ μὴ μαθεῖν.
>
> Isaiah 8:20 — Νόμον γὰρ εἰς βοήθειαν ἔδωκεν, ἵνα εἴπωσιν οὐχ ὡς τὸ ῥῆμα τοῦτο, περὶ οὗ οὐκ ἔστι δῶρα δοῦναι περὶ αὐτοῦ.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 8:16 — Then shall those who seal themselves that they may not learn the law be made manifest.
>
> Isaiah 8:20 — For he has given the law for a help, that they should not speak according to this word, concerning which there are no gifts to give for it.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The two texts have different meanings. The Hebrew of 8:16 commands the
testimony to be bound and sealed "among my disciples" (בְּלִמֻּדָי). The
Greek reads "Then shall those who seal up the law, that they may not learn
it, be made manifest." In 8:20 the Greek has "he has given the law for a
help", not the Hebrew's appeal "to the law and to the testimony".

**Notes.**

Westminster cites 8:20 (WCF 1.1, 1.8; WLC Q. 3) for the appeal to Scripture; that reading depends on the Hebrew.

### Isaiah 30:8

<a id="isa-30-8"></a>

**Claim.** Write it in a table, note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever and ever.

**Speaker:** God, through a prophet · **Role:** means · **Categories:** Inscripturation · **Object:** the prophecy against trust in Egypt

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 30:8 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">עַתָּה בּוֹא כָתְבָהּ עַל־לוּחַ אִתָּם וְעַל־סֵפֶר חֻקָּהּ וּתְהִי לְיוֹם אַחֲרוֹן לָעַד עַד־עוֹלָֽם׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 30:8 — ¶ Now go, write it before them in a table, and note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever and ever:

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 30:8 — Νῦν οὖν καθίσας γράψον ἐπὶ πυξίου ταῦτα καὶ εἰς βιβλίον, ὅτι ἔσται εἰς ἡμέρας ταῦτα καιρῷ, καὶ ἕως εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 30:8 — Now then sit down and write these words on a tablet, and in a book; for these things shall be for [many long] days, and even for ever.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew consonants לעד are pointed in the Masoretic Text as לָעַד, "for
ever", giving "for the latter day, for ever, to eternity". Read as לְעֵד, the
same letters mean "for a witness", which many modern versions adopt
**[context]**. The Greek has "for many days, and even for ever" (εἰς
ἡμέρας … καὶ ἕως εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα). The verse is an example of a reading that
turns on vowels the consonantal text does not carry.

### Isaiah 31:2

<a id="isa-31-2"></a>

**Claim.** He will bring evil, and will not call back his words.

**Speaker:** Moses or a prophet, for God · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Fulfilment · **Object:** his words

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 31:2 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וְגַם־הוּא חָכָם וַיָּבֵא רָע וְאֶת־דְּבָרָיו לֹא הֵסִיר וְקָם עַל־בֵּית מְרֵעִים וְעַל־עֶזְרַת פֹּעֲלֵי אָֽוֶן׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 31:2 — Yet he also [is] wise, and will bring evil, and will not call back his words: but will arise against the house of the evildoers, and against the help of them that work iniquity.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 31:2 — Καὶ αὐτὸς σοφῶς ἦγεν ἐπʼ αὐτοὺς κακά, καὶ ὁ λόγος αὐτοῦ οὐ μὴ ἀθετηθῇ, καὶ ἐπαναστήσεται ἐπʼ οἴκους ἀνθρώπων πονηρῶν, καὶ ἐπὶ τὴν ἐλπίδα αὐτῶν τὴν ματαίαν,

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 31:2 — Therefore he has wisely brought evils upon them, and his word shall not be frustrated; and he shall rise up against the houses of wicked men, and against their vain hope,

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew "has not removed (or turned aside) his words" (לֹא הֵסִיר) becomes "his word shall not be set aside" (οὐ μὴ ἀθετηθῇ).

### Isaiah 34:16

<a id="isa-34-16"></a>

**Claim.** Seek ye out of the book of the LORD, and read; no one of these shall fail.

**Speaker:** Moses or a prophet, for God · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Fulfilment, Inscripturation · **Object:** the book of the LORD

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 34:16 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">דִּרְשׁוּ מֵֽעַל־סֵפֶר יְהוָה וּֽקְרָאוּ אַחַת מֵהֵנָּה לֹא נֶעְדָּרָה אִשָּׁה רְעוּתָהּ לֹא פָקָדוּ כִּֽי־פִי הוּא צִוָּה וְרוּחוֹ הוּא קִבְּצָֽן׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 34:16 — ¶ Seek ye out of the book of the LORD, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth it hath commanded, and his spirit it hath gathered them.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 34:16 — Ἀριθμῷ παρῆλθον, καὶ μία αὐτῶν οὐκ ἀπώλετο· ἑτέρα τὴν ἑτέραν οὐκ ἐζήτησαν, ὅτι ὁ Κύριος αὐτοῖς ἐνετείλατο, καὶ τὸ πνεῦμα αὐτοῦ συνήγαγεν αὐτά.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 34:16 — They passed by in [full] number, and not one of them perished: they sought not one another; for the Lord commanded them, and his Spirit gathered them.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew tells the reader to "seek out of the book of the LORD, and
read". The Greek has no reference to a book: "They passed by in number,
and not one of them perished." Only the Hebrew contains the appeal to a
written book.

### Isaiah 40:6-8

<a id="isa-40-8"></a>

**Claim.** The grass withereth, the flower fadeth; but the word of our God shall stand for ever.

**Speaker:** Heavenly voice · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Permanence · **Object:** the word of our God

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 40:6 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">קוֹל אֹמֵר קְרָא וְאָמַר מָה אֶקְרָא כָּל־הַבָּשָׂר חָצִיר וְכָל־חַסְדּוֹ כְּצִיץ הַשָּׂדֶֽה׃</span>
>
> Isaiah 40:7 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">יָבֵשׁ חָצִיר נָבֵֽל צִיץ כִּי רוּחַ יְהוָה נָשְׁבָה בּוֹ אָכֵן חָצִיר הָעָֽם׃</span>
>
> Isaiah 40:8 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">יָבֵשׁ חָצִיר נָבֵֽל צִיץ וּדְבַר־אֱלֹהֵינוּ יָקוּם לְעוֹלָֽם׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 40:6 — The voice said, Cry. And he said, What shall I cry? All flesh [is] grass, and all the goodliness thereof [is] as the flower of the field:
>
> Isaiah 40:7 — The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit of the LORD bloweth upon it: surely the people [is] grass.
>
> Isaiah 40:8 — The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 40:6 — Φωνὴ λέγοντος, βόησον· καὶ εἶπα, τί βοήσω; πᾶσα σὰρξ χορτὸς, καὶ πᾶσα δόξα ἀνθρώπου ὡς ἄνθος χόρτου·
>
> Isaiah 40:7 — Ἐξηράνθη ὁ χόρτος, καὶ τὸ ἄνθος ἐξέπεσε·
>
> Isaiah 40:8 — τὸ δὲ ῥῆμα τοῦ Θεοῦ ἡμῶν μένει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 40:6 — The voice of one saying, Cry; and I said, What shall I cry? All flesh is grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of grass.
>
> Isaiah 40:7 — The grass withers, and the flower fades:
>
> Isaiah 40:8 — but the word of our God abides for ever.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

- **Length.** The Greek lacks most of verse 7 ("because the spirit of the
  LORD bloweth upon it: surely the people is grass") and the repetition
  at the start of verse 8. It reads simply: "the grass withered, and the
  flower fell; but the word of our God abides for ever."
- **Verse 6.** The Hebrew "all its goodliness" (חַסְדּוֹ) is the Greek "all
  the glory of man" (πᾶσα δόξα ἀνθρώπου).
- **The verb.** The Hebrew "shall stand" (יָקוּם) is the Greek "abides"
  (μένει).

**Used in the New Testament:** 1 Peter 1:24-25

**Notes.**

The voice of 40:6 is unidentified ("the voice said, Cry"), within an oracle
introduced as God's (40:1). 1 Peter 1:24–25 quotes the Greek, not the
Hebrew: it has the shorter text, "glory", and "abides". See the
1 Peter entry for how the Greek New Testament editions differ on this.

### Isaiah 45:23

<a id="isa-45-23"></a>

**Claim.** I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and shall not return.

**Speaker:** God, through a prophet · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Fulfilment · **Object:** the word gone out of God's mouth

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 45:23 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי יָצָא מִפִּי צְדָקָה דָּבָר וְלֹא יָשׁוּב כִּי־לִי תִּכְרַע כָּל־בֶּרֶךְ תִּשָּׁבַע כָּל־לָשֽׁוֹן׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 45:23 — I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth [in] righteousness, and shall not return, That unto me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 45:23 — Κατʼ ἐμαυτοῦ ὀμνύω, εἰ μὴ ἐξελεύσεται ἐκ τοῦ στόματός μου δικαιοσύνη, οἱ λόγοι μου οὐκ ἀποστραφήσονται· Ὅτι ἐμοὶ κάμψει πᾶν γόνυ, καὶ ὀμεῖται πᾶσα γλῶσσα τὸν Θεὸν,

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 45:23 — By myself I swear, righteousness shall surely proceed out of my mouth; my words shall not be frustrated; that to me every knee shall bend, and every tongue shall swear by God,

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew: "a word in righteousness has gone out of my mouth, and shall
not return" (וְלֹא יָשׁוּב). The Greek divides it: "righteousness shall go
out of my mouth; my words shall not be turned back" (οἱ λόγοι μου οὐκ
ἀποστραφήσονται).

**Used in the New Testament:** Romans 14:11, Philippians 2:10-11

**Notes.**

The New Testament quotes the second half (every knee shall bow), not the clause about the word.

> Isaiah 46:10 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">מַגִּיד מֵֽרֵאשִׁית אַחֲרִית וּמִקֶּדֶם אֲשֶׁר לֹא־נַעֲשׂוּ אֹמֵר עֲצָתִי תָקוּם וְכָל־חֶפְצִי אֶעֱשֶֽׂה׃</span>

> Isaiah 46:10 — telling beforehand the latter events before they come to pass, and they are accomplished together: and I said, All my counsel shall stand, and I will do all things that I have planned:
>
> Isaiah 46:11 — calling a bird from the east, and from a land afar off, for the things which I have planned: I have spoken, and brought [him]; I have created and made [him]; I have brought him, and prospered his way.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The same in substance; the Greek "all my counsel shall stand" (πᾶσα ἡ βουλή μου στήσεται).

### Isaiah 55:10-11

<a id="isa-55-11"></a>

**Claim.** My word shall not return unto me void, but shall accomplish that which I please.

**Speaker:** God, through a prophet · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Fulfilment, Efficacy · **Object:** my word that goeth forth out of my mouth

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 55:10 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כִּי כַּאֲשֶׁר יֵרֵד הַגֶּשֶׁם וְהַשֶּׁלֶג מִן־הַשָּׁמַיִם וְשָׁמָּה לֹא יָשׁוּב כִּי אִם־הִרְוָה אֶת־הָאָרֶץ וְהוֹלִידָהּ וְהִצְמִיחָהּ וְנָתַן זֶרַע לַזֹּרֵעַ וְלֶחֶם לָאֹכֵֽל׃</span>
>
> Isaiah 55:11 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כֵּן יִֽהְיֶה דְבָרִי אֲשֶׁר יֵצֵא מִפִּי לֹֽא־יָשׁוּב אֵלַי רֵיקָם כִּי אִם־עָשָׂה אֶת־אֲשֶׁר חָפַצְתִּי וְהִצְלִיחַ אֲשֶׁר שְׁלַחְתִּֽיו׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 55:10 — For as the rain cometh down, and the snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, but watereth the earth, and maketh it bring forth and bud, that it may give seed to the sower, and bread to the eater:
>
> Isaiah 55:11 — So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper [in the thing] whereto I sent it.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 55:10 — Ὡς γὰρ ἂν καταβῇ ὁ ὑετὸς ἢ χιὼν ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ, καὶ οὐ μὴ ἀποστραφῇ ἕως ἂν μεθύσῃ τὴν γῆν, καὶ ἐκτέκῃ, καὶ ἐκβλαστήσῃ, καὶ δῷ σπέρμα τῷ σπείροντι, καὶ ἄρτον εἰς βρῶσιν·
>
> Isaiah 55:11 — οὕτως ἔσται τὸ ῥῆμά μου, ὃ ἐὰν ἐξέλθῃ ἐκ τοῦ στόματός μου, οὐ μὴ ἀποστραφῇ, ἕως ἂν τελεσθῇ ὅσα ἂν ἠθέλησα, καὶ εὐοδώσω τὰς ὁδούς σου, καὶ τὰ ἐντάλματά μου.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 55:10 — For as rain shall come down, or snow, from heaven, and shall not return until it have saturated the earth, and it bring forth, and bud, and give seed to the sower, and bread for food:
>
> Isaiah 55:11 — so shall my word be, whatever shall proceed out of my mouth, it shall by no means turn back, until all the things which I willed shall have been accomplished; and I will make thy ways prosperous, and [will effect] my commands.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

The Hebrew "shall not return to me empty" (לֹא־יָשׁוּב אֵלַי רֵיקָם); Brenton's
Greek has "shall not return (οὐ μὴ ἀποστραφῇ) until all that I willed is
accomplished", without "empty", and adds "and I will prosper thy ways and
my commandments".

**Notes.**

A claim about the word's effect in the world, not about its text.

### Isaiah 59:21

<a id="isa-59-21"></a>

**Claim.** My words which I have put in thy mouth shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed, from henceforth and for ever.

**Speaker:** God, through a prophet · **Role:** claim · **Categories:** Transmission to later generations · **Object:** my spirit and my words put in thy mouth

**Hebrew (Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex)**

> Isaiah 59:21 — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַאֲנִי זֹאת בְּרִיתִי אוֹתָם אָמַר יְהוָה רוּחִי אֲשֶׁר עָלֶיךָ וּדְבָרַי אֲשֶׁר־שַׂמְתִּי בְּפִיךָ לֹֽא־יָמוּשׁוּ מִפִּיךָ וּמִפִּי זַרְעֲךָ וּמִפִּי זֶרַע זַרְעֲךָ אָמַר יְהוָה מֵעַתָּה וְעַד־עוֹלָֽם׃</span>

**English of the Hebrew (KJV, 1769)**

> Isaiah 59:21 — As for me, this [is] my covenant with them, saith the LORD; My spirit that [is] upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed’s seed, saith the LORD, from henceforth and for ever.

**Greek (Septuagint, Brenton's edition)**

> Isaiah 59:21 — Καὶ αὕτη αὐτοῖς ἡ παρʼ ἐμοῦ διαθήκη, εἶπε Κύριος· τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἐμὸν, ὅ ἐστιν ἐπὶ σοί, καὶ τὰ ῥήματα, ἃ ἔδωκα εἰς τὸ στόμα σου, οὐ μὴ ἐκλίπῃ ἐκ τοῦ στόματός σου, καὶ ἐκ τοῦ στόματος τοῦ σπέρματός σου· εἶπε γὰρ Κύριος ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν καὶ εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα.

**English of the Greek (Brenton, 1851)**

> Isaiah 59:21 — And this shall be my covenant with them, said the Lord; My Spirit which is upon thee, and the words which I have put in thy mouth, shall never fail from thy mouth, nor from the mouth of thy seed, for the Lord has spoken it, henceforth and for ever.

**Hebrew and Greek compared.**

- The Hebrew verb is plural, "they shall not depart" (לֹא־יָמוּשׁוּ, the
  spirit and the words together); the Greek is singular (οὐ μὴ ἐκλίπῃ).
- The Greek lacks "nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed".
- The Greek attaches "from henceforth and for ever" to "for the Lord has
  said it" (εἶπε γὰρ Κύριος ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν καὶ εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα).

The strongest Old Testament promise of preservation in transmission: the
words are to remain in the mouths of a succession of generations. It is a
covenant promise to the redeemed of Zion (59:20); Romans 11:27 quotes the
covenant clause. It speaks of words in mouths, not of a written copy.

**Greek (SBL Greek New Testament, CC BY 4.0)**

> Matthew 5:17 — Μὴ νομίσητε ὅτι ἦλθον καταλῦσαι τὸν νόμον ἢ τοὺς προφήτας· οὐκ ἦλθον καταλῦσαι ἀλλὰ πληρῶσαι·
>
> Matthew 5:18 — ἀμὴν γὰρ λέγω ὑμῖν, ἕως ἂν παρέλθῃ ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ἡ γῆ, ἰῶτα ἓν ἢ μία κεραία οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἀπὸ τοῦ νόμου, ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται.
>
> Matthew 5:19 — ὃς ἐὰν οὖν λύσῃ μίαν τῶν ἐντολῶν τούτων τῶν ἐλαχίστων καὶ διδάξῃ οὕτως τοὺς ἀνθρώπους, ἐλάχιστος κληθήσεται ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τῶν οὐρανῶν· ὃς δʼ ἂν ποιήσῃ καὶ διδάξῃ, οὗτος μέγας κληθήσεται ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τῶν οὐρανῶν.

**English (KJV, 1769)**

> Matthew 5:17 — ¶ Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.
>
> Matthew 5:18 — For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.
>
> Matthew 5:19 — Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven: but whosoever shall do and teach [them], the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.

**The Greek editions compared.**

The three Greek editions agree.

**Notes.**

- ἰῶτα is the Greek letter iota, the smallest letter of the alphabet the
  Gospel's readers used; κεραία, "horn", is the hook or stroke of a
  letter and, proverbially, the least detail. "One iota or one horn" is
  an idiom of totality, "not the least particle". That it stands for the
  Hebrew *yod* is a reconstruction of the saying's Aramaic background,
  not something the Greek says. **[context]**
- The verb is the one used in the same sentence of heaven and earth:
  παρέλθῃ, "pass away", cease to stand. Luke's parallel has πεσεῖν,
  "fall", the idiom for a word that loses its force (Luke 16:17).
- Verse 19 draws the consequence with "therefore" (οὖν): whoever
  "loosens" (λύσῃ) one of the least commandments. What does not pass
  away is the binding force of every part of the law, down to its least
  commandment. The saying is not about the copying of a text in any
  language. **[inferred]**, following
  [preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md §1.1](../../analysis/preservation-hebrew-and-greek.md#11-matthew-518-read-in-the-greek).
- It has two time limits: "until heaven and earth pass away" and "until
  all things take place" (ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται). Matthew 24:35 sets
  Jesus's own words beyond the first limit.
- The Westminster Confession (1.8) and the Savoy Declaration (1.8) cite
  it as the sole proof for "kept pure in all ages"; see
  [confessions.md](../../analysis/preservation-of-scripture/confessions.md#westminster-confession-18-kept-pure-in-all-ages).

> 2 Corinthians 4:2 — ἀλλὰ ἀπειπάμεθα τὰ κρυπτὰ τῆς αἰσχύνης, μὴ περιπατοῦντες ἐν πανουργίᾳ μηδὲ δολοῦντες τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ, ἀλλὰ τῇ φανερώσει τῆς ἀληθείας συνιστάνοντες ἑαυτοὺς πρὸς πᾶσαν συνείδησιν ἀνθρώπων ἐνώπιον τοῦ θεοῦ.

**English (KJV, 1769)**

> 2 Corinthians 2:17 — For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God: but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God speak we in Christ.
>
> 2 Corinthians 4:2 — But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man’s conscience in the sight of God.

**The Greek editions compared.**

In 2:17 the Byzantine text has "the rest" (οἱ λοιποί) for "many" (οἱ πολλοί).

**Notes.**

"Corrupt" is καπηλεύοντες, "peddling" (used of wine-sellers who
adulterate their wares); "handling deceitfully" is δολοῦντες,
"adulterating". Paul admits that some in his day were corrupting the word
in their handling of it.

### 2 Peter 3:16

<a id="2pet-3-16"></a>

**Claim.** The unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction.

**Speaker:** Apostle or evangelist · **Role:** warning · **Categories:** Corruption among men · **Object:** Paul's epistles and "the other scriptures"

**Greek (SBL Greek New Testament, CC BY 4.0)**

> 2 Peter 3:16 — ὡς καὶ ἐν πάσαις ταῖς ἐπιστολαῖς λαλῶν ἐν αὐταῖς περὶ τούτων, ἐν αἷς ἐστιν δυσνόητά τινα, ἃ οἱ ἀμαθεῖς καὶ ἀστήρικτοι στρεβλοῦσιν ὡς καὶ τὰς λοιπὰς γραφὰς πρὸς τὴν ἰδίαν αὐτῶν ἀπώλειαν.

**English (KJV, 1769)**

> 2 Peter 3:16 — As also in all [his] epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as [they do] also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction.

**The Greek editions compared.**

Minor; SBL reads "in which" (αἷς, the epistles) for "in which things" (οἷς).

**Notes.**
