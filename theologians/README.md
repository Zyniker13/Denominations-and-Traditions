# Theologians and teachers on the Canon

Writings of the major teachers of the Lutheran and Reformed traditions, and of
the ancient witnesses they argue from, on the canon of Scripture, the
Apocrypha, the Hebrew text and its vowel points, the Greek New Testament and
the Septuagint, and the Latin Vulgate.

These are kept apart from the confessional documents in
[`confessions/`](../confessions/README.md). The confessions bind; these texts
teach and argue. The study of them is
[analysis/canon-in-the-theologians.md](../analysis/canon-in-the-theologians.md),
the companion to
[analysis/canon-in-the-confessions.md](../analysis/canon-in-the-confessions.md).

Every text is a public-domain English edition, reproduced verbatim. Most are
**selections**: the chapters or sections that bear on these subjects, each
given complete. Each file opens with a table naming the author, the work, the
edition and translator, the source it was taken from, exactly which sections
are included, and how page numbers are marked (`[p. n]` is the page of the
edition named). Provenance for every file is also in [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md).

## Jewish antiquity

The background to the Hebrew canon and to the origin of the Septuagint.

- Josephus, [*Against Apion* I.6–8 and *Antiquities*, Preface and XII.2](jewish-antiquity/josephus/against-apion-and-antiquities.md) (Whiston): the twenty-two books; the Greek translation of the Law
- [*The Letter of Aristeas*](jewish-antiquity/aristeas/letter-of-aristeas.md) (Thackeray, 1917): the seventy-two translators, selections
- Philo, [*On the Life of Moses* II](jewish-antiquity/philo/life-of-moses.md) (Yonge, 1855): the translators "like men inspired"

## The early church

The witnesses every later tradition appeals to. Translations from the
*Ante-Nicene* and *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers* (1885–1900) unless noted.

- Eusebius, [*Church History*](early-church/eusebius/church-history.md): Melito's and Origen's lists; the accepted, disputed and rejected books (III.3, 10, 24, 25, 38; IV.26; V.8; VI.14, 16, 25, 31; VII.25)
- Origen and Julius Africanus, [letters on Susanna](early-church/origen/letter-to-africanus.md): the Hebrew and the Greek additions to Daniel
- Athanasius, [Festal Letter 39](early-church/athanasius/festal-letter-39.md) (367): the canon and the books "to be read"
- Cyril of Jerusalem, [*Catechetical Lecture* IV.33–36](early-church/cyril-of-jerusalem/catechetical-lecture-4.md)
- [Canon lists of the councils](early-church/councils/canon-lists.md): Apostolical Canon 85, Laodicea 59–60, Carthage (419) 24, Trullo 2
- Rufinus, [*Commentary on the Creed* 36–38 and *Apology* II.32–37](early-church/rufinus/creed-and-apology.md): canonical, "ecclesiastical" and apocryphal books; the defence of the Septuagint
- Jerome:
  - [Prefaces to the Vulgate](early-church/jerome/vulgate-prefaces.md), including the *Prologus Galeatus*
  - [Letters 53, 57, 71 and 107](early-church/jerome/letters.md)
  - [*Apology against Rufinus* II.24–35](early-church/jerome/apology-against-rufinus.md)
- Augustine:
  - [*On Christian Doctrine* II](early-church/augustine/on-christian-doctrine.md): the canon of forty-four books; translations
  - [*City of God*](early-church/augustine/city-of-god.md) XV, XVII.20, XVIII.36–44: the Septuagint and the Apocrypha
  - [Letters with Jerome](early-church/augustine/letters-with-jerome.md) (28, 71, 75, 82): translating from the Hebrew
- Gregory the Great, [*Moralia* XIX.21](early-church/gregory-the-great/moralia.md) (Oxford, 1845): Maccabees "though not Canonical"
- John of Damascus, [*Exact Exposition* IV.17](early-church/john-of-damascus/exact-exposition.md)

- Henry Eyster Jacobs:
  - [*A Summary of the Christian Faith*, Holy Scripture](lutheran/henry-eyster-jacobs/summary-of-the-christian-faith-holy-scripture.md) (1905)

## Not included

No public-domain English text could be found for Turretin's *Institutes*
(Topic II), Gerhard's *Loci* and Chemnitz's *Examination of the Council of
Trent* (both present here only through Schmid), Luther's prefaces to the
Apocrypha, the Leiden *Synopsis*, Polanus, or the Latin writers on the vowel
points (Cappellus, the Buxtorfs). Walton, Lightfoot, Ussher and Leigh have not
yet been attempted. See the
[Gaps](../analysis/canon-in-the-theologians.md#gaps) section of the analysis.
